# Decodability Is Not Controllability: Local and Anisotropic Speaker Control in Continuous-Latent TTS

Yuanzhong Zheng    Wei Huang    Ming Ma    Kailun Jin    Youhai
Jiang\sthanksCorresponding author

###### Abstract

High linear decodability does not entail causal controllability in
continuous-latent text-to-speech (TTS). In VoxCPM2, position-matched
probes and equal-norm interventions across 30 speakers and 60 directed
dyads expose this separation: reference-audio states at the final
residual acoustic language model layer (RALM L7) retain 0.970 probe
accuracy, yet aligned edits yield 0/60 target-dominant dyads. Edits at
early reference-audio positions produce the opposite pattern. At L0,
pair-aligned updates increase target similarity by 0.326 and achieve
target dominance in all 60 dyads, against at most 1/60 under any
equal-norm direction or position control. Independent speaker encoders,
retrieval against held-out speaker prototypes, and double-blind
listening confirm that the shifted voice is heard as the intended
target. Cross-architecture interventions in dots.tts distinguish
backbone leverage from direct acoustic control: backbone edits displace
the source without target attraction, while x-vector ablation reduces
source similarity from 0.849 to 0.006. These findings identify two
requirements for training-free speaker control: geometric specificity
and a computational route that carries the intended speaker condition
into acoustic synthesis.

###### Index Terms: 

text-to-speech, speaker control, causal intervention, linear probes,
representation analysis

## 1 Introduction

Prompt-conditioned text-to-speech (TTS) systems synthesize target voices
from reference audio \[1, 2, 3, 4, 5\]. While linear probes measure the
speaker information available in hidden states \[6, 7\], the critical
distinction lies between reading that information and using it to
control the generated voice \[8, 9, 10\]. We expose a direct
counterexample to selecting control sites by probe accuracy: VoxCPM2’s
L7 reference-audio states retain 0.970 accuracy yet yield 0/60
target-dominant dyads under aligned intervention, whereas L0 aligned
edits yield 60/60. Locating speaker control therefore requires attending
to both the direction of an edit and its route into synthesis.

Activation patching, causal mediation, and representation steering test
model behavior under direct intervention \[11, 12, 13, 14, 15\]. In
speech synthesis, recent work uses activation steering to manipulate
attributes such as emotion, style, and accent \[16, 17, 18, 19\]. We
couple position-matched probing with equal-norm geometric controls to
localize speaker control and distinguish target attraction from acoustic
disruption.

Target-specific control requires both increasing similarity to target
speaker $`B`$ and making the output closer to $`B`$ than to source
speaker $`A`$. An update can also suppress the source voice or disrupt
speech without producing the target voice. We distinguish decodability,
the ability to predict speaker identity from a state; leverage, the
effect of changing that state on speech; and specificity, whether the
change steers speech toward the intended target.

We study these properties in the residual acoustic language model (RALM)
prefill states of VoxCPM2 \[1\]. Across 30 English LibriTTS-R speakers
\[20\] organized into 60 role-balanced directed dyads (evaluated over
two prompts and three generation seeds), we vary layer depth, token
position, and perturbation direction in an equal-norm factorial.
Independent speaker encoders, retrieval against held-out speaker
prototypes, and double-blind listening measure the identity of the
generated voice.

First, the experiments establish a sharp dissociation between
decodability and target control: L0 reference-audio edits achieve target
dominance in all 60 dyads, whereas L7 edits yield 0/60 despite a probe
accuracy of 0.970. Second, control is anisotropic and position-specific:
pair-aligned updates at early reference-audio positions produce target
attraction, whereas equal-norm orthogonal updates reveal
source-displacing leverage, and final-state updates primarily affect
intelligibility. The comparison isolates the directions and positions
that give leverage its target specificity. Finally, readout reach and
direct conditioning identify the architectural routes for speaker
control. In VoxCPM2, subsequent attention blocks carry early
reference-state edits to the synthesis readout. In dots.tts, backbone
edits and x-vector ablation distinguish behavioral leverage from a
source-identity anchor supplied directly to the acoustic head. These
experiments identify the editable states and conditioning inputs that a
speaker-control interface must address.

## 2 Method

### 2.1 Model and probe

VoxCPM2 encodes reference waveforms into AudioVAE patches, maps them
through a local audio encoder, interleaves the resulting audio
embeddings with text embeddings, and processes the sequence through a
TSLM and an eight-layer RALM; a flow-matching LocDiT and the AudioVAE
decoder then synthesize the waveform \[1\]. Interventions modify
post-block RALM hidden states at prefill step 0 and retain the
intervened block’s already-computed key/value (KV) cache. Subsequent
autoregressive steps use the standard forward computation. Early-block
updates propagate through later RALM blocks and their KV caches. The
final RALM readout selects the last sequence position to condition
LocDiT. We define attention-mediated readout reach as the capacity of a
reference-audio intervention to influence the final conditioning state
through subsequent RALM attention blocks. Early reference-audio edits
have this route; post-block L7 reference-audio edits lie beyond the
final mixing block. Edits to the final sequence position at L7 reach the
acoustic generator directly.

The matched probe contains 300 held-out utterances from the same
30-speaker universe as the causal experiment. At layers L0, L1, L2, L3,
L5, and L7, a linear classifier predicts speaker identity from the
reference-audio mean, non-audio-token mean, final state, or
whole-sequence mean. We use five-fold cross-validation grouped by
transcript identity and 1,000 full-label permutations with a
24-condition max-statistic family-wise correction. The probe matches the
causal experiment’s speaker pool and reference/enrollment exclusions.

For source $`A`$, target $`B`$, shared synthesis text $`x`$, and RALM
layer $`\ell`$, let $`h_{A}^{\ell}`$ and $`h_{B}^{\ell}`$ be post-block
prefill states. Before subtraction, $`h_{B}^{\ell}`$ is length-aligned
to the source sequence by linear interpolation when shorter and
truncation when longer. The pair-specific direction is

```math
d_{A\rightarrow B}^{\ell}=h_{B}^{\ell}-h_{A}^{\ell}. \tag{1}
```

Let $`M_{p}`$ be a binary mask selecting position type $`p`$, broadcast
across channels, and let $`z^{\ell}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)`$ have the same
shape as $`h_{A}^{\ell}`$. For direction condition
$`k\in\{\mathrm{align},\mathrm{orth},\mathrm{rand}\}`$, define
$`u_{p,\mathrm{align}}^{\ell}=M_{p}d_{A\rightarrow B}^{\ell}`$,
$`u_{p,\mathrm{rand}}^{\ell}=M_{p}z^{\ell}`$, and
$`u_{p,\mathrm{orth}}^{\ell}=M_{p}z^{\ell}-\operatorname{proj}_{u_{p,\mathrm{align}}^{\ell}}(M_{p}z^{\ell})`$,
where
$`\operatorname{proj}_{a}(b)=\langle b,a\rangle_{F}a/\|a\|_{F}^{2}`$.
With reference-audio aligned norm
$`r^{\ell}=\|u_{\mathrm{ref},\mathrm{align}}^{\ell}\|_{F}`$, every
condition uses

```math
\widetilde{h}_{A}^{\ell}=h_{A}^{\ell}+r^{\ell}\frac{u_{p,k}^{\ell}}{\|u_{p,k}^{\ell}\|_{F}}. \tag{2}
```

At L0, L3, and L7, the factorial tests reference-audio aligned,
reference-audio orthogonal, reference-audio random, non-audio aligned,
and final-state aligned conditions. The non-audio mask contains BOS,
separator, and text positions; the final-state mask contains only the
last position. For each dyad and prompt at a given layer, all five
conditions share $`r^{\ell}`$, matching total Frobenius norm across
directions and positions.

Probe accuracy measures decodability; changes in source similarity and
WER measure behavioral leverage; target gain and target dominance
measure specificity. For generated speech $`y`$, the primary metric is
matched-baseline target gain

```math
\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{B}=\mathrm{SIM}(y_{\mathrm{int}},B)-\mathrm{SIM}(y_{\mathrm{base}},B), \tag{3}
```

where SIM is cosine similarity between L2-normalized speaker embeddings.
After averaging seeds within prompts and the two prompts within each
directed dyad, a dyad satisfies the target-dominant criterion when both
$`\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{B}>0`$ and
$`\mathrm{SIM}(y_{\mathrm{int}},B)>\mathrm{SIM}(y_{\mathrm{int}},A)`$.
The criterion combines target attraction with target-over-source
preference in the same dyad. The primary factorial uses ECAPA-TDNN
\[21\]. We score the same generated speech with CAM++ \[22\], perform
30-way retrieval against five-utterance speaker prototypes disjoint from
all intervention references, and use Whisper WER \[23\] as an
intelligibility measure.

The directed dyad is the inferential unit. We report dyad-bootstrap 95%
confidence intervals and two-way source/target cluster-robust 95%
confidence intervals accounting for repeated speakers in both roles
\[24\].

## 3 Results

### 3.1 Distributed speaker information, localized target control

The depth contrast separates speaker information from target control: L0
aligned reference-audio edits achieve target dominance in 60/60 dyads,
while L7 edits yield 0/60 despite a probe accuracy of 0.970. Mean target
gain falls from $`\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{B}=+0.326`$ at L0 to $`0.000`$ at
L7; L3 retains a gain of $`+0.227`$ with 52/60 target-dominant dyads
(Table 1). The final layer thus retains decodable speaker information
after reference-audio edits have lost attention-mediated readout reach.

Speaker information is distributed more broadly than this control
region. Reference-audio probe accuracy stays within 0.947–0.973 across
layers, while non-audio accuracy rises from 0.223 at L0 to 0.903 at L7
and final-state accuracy from 0.637 to 0.933. All reported probe
conditions satisfy $`p_{\mathrm{FWE}}=0.001`$ under the 24-condition
permutation correction. A finer-grained six-layer sweep over 25 dyads
resolves the early control region spanning L0–L3, followed by a sharp
decline at L5 (Fig. 1(a)); target dominance reaches 0/25 at L5 and L7.

### 3.2 Direction and position determine the steering response

Target specificity depends on both update direction and token position.
At L0, pair-aligned reference-audio steering exceeds equal-norm
orthogonal and random updates by $`+0.342`$ and $`+0.340`$ in target
similarity, respectively (Fig. 1(b)). It also exceeds non-audio and
final-state aligned interventions by $`+0.318`$ and $`+0.306`$. All four
95% confidence intervals lie above zero under both dyad-bootstrap and
two-way source/target cluster-robust inference. At L3,
aligned-minus-control gains range from $`+0.189`$ to $`+0.235`$; at L7,
aligned and direction-control contrasts are centered near zero.

At L0, aligned reference-audio intervention both displaces the source
and attracts the target ($`\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{A}=-0.467`$;
post-intervention $`\mathrm{SIM}_{B}=0.467>\mathrm{SIM}_{A}=0.173`$).
Orthogonal perturbations reduce source similarity
($`\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{A}=-0.195`$) while also reducing target
similarity ($`\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{B}=-0.016`$; 95% CI excludes zero).
Orthogonal edits exert behavioral leverage by moving speech away from
both speakers; pair alignment channels that leverage toward the intended
target. Final-state interventions concentrate their leverage in
intelligibility, raising matched-baseline $`\Delta\mathrm{WER}`$ by
$`+0.113`$ \[$`+0.058`$, $`+0.179`$\] at L0, $`+0.127`$ \[$`+0.043`$,
$`+0.251`$\] at L3, and $`+0.096`$ \[$`+0.053`$, $`+0.147`$\] at L7,
with low target-dominant counts (Table 1). Reference-audio aligned
intervention achieves target gains with mean $`\Delta\mathrm{WER}`$ near
zero (Table 2). Across the same 25 dyads used for the six-layer sweep,
an L0 norm sweep at $`0.5,1,2,5\times`$ the native norm yields
$`+0.186,+0.387,+0.302,+0.342`$. The aligned direction supports target
attraction at all four scales, spanning a tenfold norm range, with the
largest gain at the native norm.

### 3.3 Target specificity extends to held-out speaker references

The target-speaker match extends across evaluators and reference
utterances. Independent CAM++ evaluation reproduces the depth ordering:
aligned target gain is $`+0.237,+0.190,+0.001`$ at L0/L3/L7, with 55/60,
43/60, and 0/60 target-dominant dyads (Table 2). We also compare
generated speech with five-utterance prototypes disjoint from the
intervention references. At L0, ECAPA and CAM++ both retrieve the
intended target as top-1 among 30 candidates for 46/60 dyads; at L3 this
is 44/60 and 41/60, respectively, while at L7 it falls to 0/60 for both.
Successful retrieval against these independent, multi-utterance
prototypes establishes that target-speaker matching generalizes beyond
the intervention reference to the speaker level.

### 3.4 Direct acoustic conditioning anchors speaker identity in dots.tts

To trace control across distinct conditioning paths, we apply the
direction-control protocol to dots.tts \[25\], a 2B-parameter continuous
autoregressive TTS model with a 28-layer backbone. Its acoustic head
receives a global speaker x-vector \[26\] alongside backbone states.
Speakers, dyads, prompts, seeds, and evaluators are matched to the
primary factorial. We probe and intervene at backbone layers L0, L14,
and L27 with aligned, orthogonal, and random directions at reference
positions.

X-vector ablation pinpoints a strong source-identity anchor at the
direct acoustic input: disabling it reduces mean source similarity from
0.849 to 0.006. The x-vector and backbone states reach the acoustic head
through separate routes; backbone interventions edit one route while
keeping the source-speaker x-vector fixed.

The backbone itself carries both decodable identity and behavioral
leverage. Final-state probe accuracy increases with depth, from 0.260 at
L0 to 0.437 at L14 and 0.530 at L27, measured by five-fold
cross-validation. Early aligned intervention is direction-specific
(Table 3): $`\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{A}=-0.197`$ at L0 versus $`-0.032`$ and
$`-0.030`$ for equal-norm orthogonal and random directions, with a
weaker aligned effect at L27 ($`-0.046`$). CAM++ reproduces the aligned
source-displacement profile ($`-0.154,-0.155,-0.023`$ at L0/L14/L27). L0
aligned Whisper WER is 0.475, versus 0.107 for orthogonal and 0.089 for
random updates. Mean $`\Delta\mathrm{SIM}_{B}`$ is non-positive in every
condition, and all conditions yield 0/60 target-dominant dyads. This
contrast separates behavioral leverage from target specificity within
the same backbone. The acoustic head receives edited backbone states
alongside a source-speaker x-vector that remains fixed. Backbone
intervention and x-vector ablation therefore act on different
conditioning routes: one reveals effects on source similarity and
intelligibility; the other identifies the direct input that anchors
speaker identity.

### 3.5 Listeners hear the target-speaker shift

Human judgments evaluate five comparisons over the 60 dyads. Each
comparison receives five double-blind judgments per dyad (300 per
comparison; 1,500 total), with candidate order randomized and
counterbalanced. ABX analysis uses all 300 trials per comparison in a
binary mixed-effects logistic regression (tested-condition preference =
1; alternative or tie = 0), with random intercepts for dyads and
listeners. Comparative naturalness uses a seven-point CMOS scale from
$`-3`$ to $`+3`$ (intervention minus matched baseline).

With an unseen target-speaker utterance as reference, listeners prefer
L0 aligned over its equal-norm orthogonal control in 72.3% of trials, L3
aligned over its orthogonal control in 63.7%, and L0 aligned over L7
aligned in 76.0%; all three contrasts are significant at $`p<0.001`$
(Table 4). Listeners hear the target specificity of early aligned
steering while baseline naturalness is largely preserved: mean
$`\Delta\mathrm{CMOS}`$ is $`-0.12`$ (95% CI $`[-0.21,-0.03]`$) at L0
and $`-0.07`$ ($`[-0.15,+0.01]`$) at L3.

## 4 Discussion and Conclusion

Speaker decodability and target control separate sharply under
intervention: states that decode speaker identity almost perfectly can
lie beyond the mixing path that reaches synthesis, while early
reference-audio states admit precise, target-directed edits. Equal-norm
controls identify the directions and positions responsible for this
response. Held-out retrieval and listening judgments connect the
resulting target specificity to speaker identity and perception.

The mechanism combines geometric specificity with readout reach.
Subsequent attention blocks carry early reference-audio edits to
VoxCPM2’s synthesis readout; post-block L7 reference-audio edits lie
beyond this mixing path. The dots.tts comparison reveals a different
routing arrangement: a direct x-vector input anchors source identity
alongside an editable backbone with behavioral leverage. These findings
give a concrete design principle for training-free speaker control:
expose editable states with downstream readout reach, and coordinate
their target-directed updates with any direct acoustic
speaker-conditioning inputs. Control-site selection must account for
both intervention geometry and the paths that carry speaker identity
into synthesis.
