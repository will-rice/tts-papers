###### Abstract

Latent text-to-speech (TTS) systems generate codec latents decoded by a
frozen decoder, but good reconstruction does not ensure geometry
suitable for Flow generation from isotropic Gaussian noise. We present
Embedded Acoustic Flow TTS (EAF-TTS), an acoustic-latent adaptation of
ELF with phone- and duration-conditioned generation, shared-network
terminal correction, and frozen WaveVAE decoding. EAF-TTS supports a
fixed ZCA interface estimated from training data and adding no learned
parameters. The codec, conditioning modules, Flow backbone, and 20-call
inference are shared. The intervention is the full ZCA coordinate
protocol—training and sampling coordinates, induced loss metric, and
terminal perturbation—not an inference-only transform. In single-speaker
LJSpeech, the 64-D training-latent covariance has condition number
1355.44. On text-disjoint Val399 strict-GT with fixed E100 raw
checkpoints across three matched seeds, ZCA improves UTMOS22 Strong—an
automatic naturalness proxy, not human MOS—by $`+0.405\pm 0.027`$ over
Scalar normalization (mean $`\pm`$ seed SD of per-seed paired
differences). Absolute scores remain below codec-reference levels.
Without retraining, ZCA-5 retains $`+0.375\pm 0.027`$ UTMOS over
Scalar-20 while reducing generation-path RTF by $`3.34\times`$. This
three-seed analysis shows no statistically detectable WER or exact-match
degradation, but does not establish equivalence. ZCA-3 gives
$`5.03\times`$ with a $`+0.165`$ UTMOS advantage. At matched reduced
call counts, ZCA’s advantage shrinks, indicating full-step headroom
rather than better low-step integration. A secondary text-only
selected-checkpoint result is directionally consistent but confounded by
checkpoint type. We claim only proxy-naturalness and measured
quality–latency effects within this protocol; human listening, blind
text, and broader validation remain necessary.

_Preprint. UTMOS is treated throughout as an automatic proxy for
naturalness, not as human MOS. The repeatedly accessed Test399 split is
used only for diagnosis and is not presented as a blind test set._

Keywords: text-to-speech; flow matching; codec-latent TTS; latent
interface; latent whitening; ZCA; proxy naturalness; sampling efficiency

## 1 Introduction

Latent generative models compress high-rate waveforms into low-rate
continuous representations and learn a conditional distribution over
them, shortening the generated sequence and leaving fine waveform
reconstruction to a separately trained neural audio codec. Codec-latent
generation has proved effective in NaturalSpeech 2 \[1\]. Separately,
Matcha-TTS, Voicebox, and F5-TTS have demonstrated efficient and
scalable Flow Matching for speech synthesis \[2, 3, 4\]. Building on
Embedded Language Flows (ELF) \[5\], we introduce Embedded Acoustic Flow
TTS (EAF-TTS), an acoustic-latent adaptation with two shared-network
modes: mode 0 performs phone- and duration-conditioned Flow generation,
and mode 1 performs one terminal clean-latent correction before frozen
WaveVAE decoding. EAF-TTS is the platform for the controlled coordinate
study below.

This modular design introduces an interface problem. A codec is trained
for reconstruction, not for isotropic Gaussian priors, straight
probability paths, or Euclidean Flow Matching objectives. Good
reconstruction establishes $`D(E(s))\approx s`$ for waveform $`s`$,
where $`E`$ and $`D`$ denote the codec encoder and decoder, but it does
not establish that $`E(s)`$ is well conditioned for conditional Flow
generation. The codec need not itself be trained for these generative
objectives; the question is whether a post-hoc coordinate protocol can
compensate. On the repeatedly accessed Test399 diagnostic set, original
audio and frozen-WaveVAE reconstructions obtain UTMOS22 Strong scores of
4.372 and 4.259 and ASR WERs of 2.885% and 2.856%, respectively. In the
seed-42 fixed-E100-raw strict-GT diagnostic run, the Scalar Flow obtains
3.162% WER but only 2.785 UTMOS22 Strong. This contrast provides
diagnostic evidence consistent with localizing the observed
proxy-naturalness gap to conditional latent generation. These numbers
motivate the question; they are non-blind and are not used to estimate
the ZCA effect, which is evaluated on Val399 with fixed E100 raw
checkpoints.

The covariance of the 64-dimensional LJSpeech training latents motivates
the intervention: it has condition number 1355.44, with eigenvalue range
$`[0.0061128,8.28546]`$. Scalar normalization corrects only global mean
and scale, leaving channel correlation and principal-direction
anisotropy intact. ZCA is a canonical symmetric whitening choice \[6\],
but the frozen decoder’s directional sensitivity need not align with
data covariance. We therefore treat ZCA as a complete coordinate
protocol and audit its decoder-domain and optimization effects rather
than assuming that a lower condition number is sufficient.

We ask a deliberately narrow question:

> Under a frozen codec and a shared generative architecture, does the
> full ZCA coordinate protocol, estimated only from training data,
> reproducibly improve UTMOS22 Strong proxy naturalness across three
> matched seeds on text-disjoint Val399 under strict ground-truth
> durations?

Scalar and ZCA systems use the same WaveVAE, text and phone front ends,
duration model, EAF-TTS backbone, training budget, matched random seeds,
and, for the primary comparison, the same inference settings. The
intervention is the full ZCA coordinate protocol: a fixed
train-data-estimated transform with no learned parameters, its induced
Euclidean loss metric when expressed in codec coordinates, and the
coordinate-space perturbation used to train mode 1. The transform is
invertible up to audited numerical precision. We interpret the results
as the aggregate effect of this protocol, not as evidence that reducing
the condition number alone is causal. The primary analysis compares
fixed E100 non-EMA weights across three matched seeds. A zero-retraining
call-count sweep is a deployment audit rather than a pure coordinate
comparison. A secondary text-only comparison is also non-causal and
additionally confounded by its use of Scalar EMA and ZCA raw
checkpoints.

We do not claim novelty in Flow Matching, codec latents, explicit
durations, or whitening/ZCA. EAF-TTS is a specific acoustic-latent
adaptation of ELF; the primary empirical contribution is the controlled
treatment of codec-latent second-order geometry within this system. Our
contributions are:

1.  1.  EAF-TTS. We introduce an acoustic-latent adaptation of ELF that
        combines phone- and duration-conditioned continuous generation,
        shared-network terminal correction, and frozen WaveVAE decoding.
2.  2.  Diagnostic localization. Non-blind reconstruction diagnostics
        provide evidence consistent with localizing the observed
        proxy-naturalness gap to conditional latent generation, rather than
        to frozen-codec reconstruction, under the studied protocol.
3.  3.  Controlled full-step ZCA effect. We evaluate the full ZCA coordinate
        protocol with latent round-trip and waveform-reconstruction audits.
        On Val399 strict-GT with fixed E100 raw checkpoints, it improves
        UTMOS22 Strong by $`+0.405\pm 0.027`$ over Scalar across three
        matched seeds, while WER, exact-match rate, and DTW-MFCC do not
        improve consistently.
4.  4.  Few-call and boundary evidence. ZCA-5 retains $`+0.375\pm 0.027`$
        UTMOS over Scalar-20 while reducing measured strict-GT
        generation-path RTF by $`3.34\times`$. The fixed-three-seed analysis
        finds no statistically detectable WER or exact-match degradation,
        but does not establish equivalence. At every matched reduced call
        count, ZCA’s advantage over Scalar is smaller than at 20 calls,
        indicating usable full-step headroom rather than better low-step
        integration. The confounded text-only comparison and negative
        decoder and gradient audits are retained as secondary boundary
        evidence.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Flow Matching for acoustic latent generation

Flow Matching trains continuous normalizing flows by regressing a vector
field along a prescribed probability path \[7\]. Matcha-TTS applies
optimal-transport conditional Flow Matching to mel-spectrogram
generation in a non-autoregressive TTS architecture \[2\]; we treat
explicit duration conditioning as a fixed system component rather than
as a contribution. Voicebox and F5-TTS demonstrate the scalability of
Flow Matching for large speech-generation systems \[3, 4\].

We introduce EAF-TTS as our acoustic-latent adaptation of Embedded
Language Flows (ELF) \[5\]. ELF performs most generation in continuous
token-embedding space and switches to a shared-weight decoding mode only
at the endpoint. EAF-TTS transfers this pattern to continuous acoustic
latents with two shared-network modes: mode 0 performs phone- and
duration-conditioned Flow generation, whereas mode 1 makes one terminal
clean-latent correction before frozen WaveVAE decoding. To the best of
our knowledge, this specific ELF-to-TTS adaptation has not previously
been described. EAF-TTS does not introduce a new general Flow objective;
it instantiates ELF’s shared generation/terminal-correction pattern in
an acoustic-latent TTS system.

### 2.2 Codec-latent generation

Neural audio codecs such as SoundStream show that low-rate
representations can preserve high-quality waveform information \[8\].
NaturalSpeech 2 performs speech and singing generation with codec-latent
diffusion \[1\]. SupertonicTTS combines continuous speech-autoencoder
latents, Flow-Matching text-to-latent generation, and an utterance-level
duration predictor \[9\]. Beyond neural audio codecs, LAFMA studies Flow
Matching on continuous mel-spectrogram VAE latents for text-to-audio
generation \[10\], while Flow Matching in Latent Space studies Flow
generation in continuous image-autoencoder representations \[11\].
EAF-TTS differs by adapting ELF’s shared-network
generation/terminal-correction pattern to phone- and
duration-conditioned WaveVAE latents. The present experiment then
freezes that architecture and isolates a global second-order
(covariance) coordinate intervention.

### 2.3 Whitening and preconditioning

Whitening centers data and applies an inverse covariance square root.
Whitening matrices are not unique. For a positive-definite covariance,
ZCA uses its unique symmetric positive-definite inverse square root and,
among whitening transforms, minimizes the expected squared distance
between centered original and whitened variables \[6\]. Decorrelated
Batch Normalization and VAE whitening show that whitening can alter
optimization and representation factorization \[12, 13\]. Preconditioned
diffusion sampling uses matrix preconditioning to mitigate
ill-conditioned sampling geometry \[14\], whereas conditionally whitened
generative models incorporate conditional means and covariances into
diffusion and Flow Matching \[15\].

These results do not guarantee that whitening codec latents will improve
TTS in general. A frozen decoder induces a decoder-domain geometry that
need not match data covariance, and a coordinate transform changes both
squared losses and decoder-domain gradients. We therefore pair positive
generation results with audits that constrain a simple “lower condition
number is sufficient” explanation.

## 3 Method

### 3.1 Embedded Acoustic Flow TTS

The frozen WaveVAE encodes waveform $`s`$ as

```math
z=E(s),\qquad z\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 64},
```

where $`T`$ is the acoustic frame count shared by the codec latent and
the frame-level condition.

Text is normalized and converted to ARPA phone symbols with a lexicon
and a grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) module. Following the explicit
length-expansion paradigm of FastSpeech-style systems \[16\], in which
phone durations are predicted before acoustic generation, a frozen
duration model predicts total duration, phone durations, and pauses. A
length regulator expands phone representations into frame-level
condition $`C\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times h}`$. The acoustic sequence length
and phone timestamps are therefore determined before ODE integration.
Figure 1 summarizes the full pipeline and the placement of the
coordinate interfaces.

This adaptation has three practical properties. First, generation
operates on the codec’s low-rate latent sequence rather than at waveform
rate, leaving fine waveform reconstruction to the frozen decoder.
Second, explicit durations determine the latent length and phone
timestamps before integration. Third, mode 1 reuses the mode 0 backbone
for terminal correction and therefore does not introduce a separate
large refiner. These are architectural properties; the present study
does not isolate their individual causal effects.

Modes 0 and 1 share the latent input and output projections, positional
representation, phone encoder, frame and speaker conditioning, DiT
blocks, and output head. A learned two-entry mode embedding, added to
the time embedding, selects the behavior. Mode 1’s latent, mel, and STFT
losses update these shared EAF-TTS parameters. WaveVAE parameters remain
frozen, while gradients propagate through its decoder from the mel and
STFT objectives to the predicted latent.

Figure 1: ZCA EAF-TTS with train-data-only statistics. Orange modules
are fixed ZCA-related coordinate interfaces with no learned parameters;
blue modules are shared or frozen components. Scalar and ZCA systems
share the text front end, duration model, EAF backbone, and frozen
WaveVAE. The fixed ZCA statistics are estimated from training data only.

For a target latent in the chosen coordinate system, denoted
$`x\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 64}`$ (see Section 3.2 for the Scalar and ZCA
choices), a Gaussian source $`\epsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{T\times 64}`$ with
$`\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)`$, and time $`t\in(0.01,0.99)`$ drawn
from the logit-normal schedule shared by the Scalar and ZCA mode 0
systems (Section 4.3), the training path is

```math
x_{t}=tx+(1-t)\epsilon.
```

The shared mode 0 network predicts a clean endpoint
$`\hat{x}_{\theta}`$. Training minimizes a time-weighted
endpoint-prediction loss, averaged over valid frames:

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FM}}=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\frac{\lVert\hat{x}_{\theta}(x_{t},t,C)-x\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\left(1-\min(t,0.95)+10^{-5}\right)^{2}}\right].
```

The time cap limits the maximum near-terminal weight to approximately
$`400`$, even when the sampled $`t`$ exceeds $`0.95`$. The velocity is
constructed only for numerical integration at inference:

```math
v_{\theta}(x_{t},t)=\frac{\hat{x}_{\theta}-x_{t}}{1-t}.
```

Primary full-step inference uses 20 model calls: 19 mode 0 Euler updates
from $`t=0`$ to $`t_{\mathrm{end}}=0.95`$ with
$`\Delta t=t_{\mathrm{end}}/19=0.05`$, followed by one mode 1
clean-latent correction at $`t=1`$. The endpoint
$`t_{\mathrm{end}}=0.95`$ is a stability choice rather than a
theoretical requirement. Because velocity reconstruction divides by
$`1-t`$, a fixed clean-endpoint prediction error is amplified by
approximately $`20\times`$ at $`t=0.95`$ and $`100\times`$ at
$`t=0.99`$, while the expression is undefined at $`t=1`$. An exact
predictor would cancel the numerator in the limit, but finite prediction
errors can destabilize near-terminal integration under explicit
integrators such as Euler. We therefore stop mode 0 integration at
$`t_{\mathrm{end}}`$ and use mode 1 as a learned terminal correction
that directly predicts the clean latent without constructing a velocity.
Section 4.4 evaluates fewer Euler updates at the same endpoint without
retraining.

On the ideal interpolation path,
$`x_{t_{\mathrm{end}}}=t_{\mathrm{end}}x+(1-t_{\mathrm{end}})\epsilon`$
retains only a $`0.05`$ source-noise coefficient and is therefore close
to the clean endpoint. Mode 1 is trained in each system’s active Flow
coordinate on clean latents with small Gaussian perturbations applied to
a subset of frames. The numeric perturbation scale is 0.1 for both
Scalar and ZCA, but the same coordinate-space magnitude does not imply
the same codec-space perturbation after their different inverse
transforms. The primary comparison therefore includes this
coordinate-dependent mode 1 training effect. The generated state at
$`t_{\mathrm{end}}`$ is intended to lie in the near-clean input regime,
although the learned ODE state need not follow the ideal path exactly
and the training and inference input distributions are not assumed to
match. Mode 1 uses latent, mel, and STFT auxiliary losses with the
shared weights reported in Section 4.3 and requires no clean target at
inference.

### 3.2 Scalar and ZCA coordinates

The Scalar baseline applies a single global scalar mean
$`\mu_{s}\in\mathbb{R}`$ and scalar standard deviation
$`\sigma_{s}\in\mathbb{R}`$, estimated jointly over all valid training
frames and all 64 channels:

```math
x_{s}=\frac{z-\mu_{s}}{\sigma_{s}}.
```

Let $`\bm{\Sigma}`$ denote the channel covariance of the original codec
latents, estimated over valid training frames. Scalar normalization
changes it only to $`\bm{\Sigma}/\sigma_{s}^{2}`$, so the covariance
condition number remains $`1355.44`$ and the eigenvectors are unchanged;
channel correlations and principal-direction anisotropy are therefore
preserved.

ZCA instead uses the same $`N`$ valid training frames, with each row
vector $`z_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 64}`$, to estimate the channel mean
and population covariance (normalized by $`N`$, not $`N-1`$):

```math
\bm{\mu}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}z_{n},\qquad\bm{\Sigma}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(z_{n}-\bm{\mu})^{\top}(z_{n}-\bm{\mu}),\quad\bm{\Sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{64\times 64}.
```

Using the row-vector convention and the eigendecomposition
$`\bm{\Sigma}=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}`$, we stabilize the eigenvalues as
$`\tilde{\lambda}_{i}=\max(\lambda_{i},10^{-5}\lambda_{\max},10^{-8})`$,
where $`10^{-5}\lambda_{\max}`$ is a relative floor and $`10^{-8}`$ is
an absolute floor, and write
$`\tilde{\Lambda}=\operatorname{diag}(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})`$. For
LJSpeech, the raw eigenvalue range is $`[0.0061128,8.28546]`$, giving
$`8.28546/0.0061128\approx 1355.4`$. The relative floor is
$`8.28546\times 10^{-5}`$ and the absolute floor is $`10^{-8}`$; both
are below $`\lambda_{\min}`$, and no eigenvalue is clamped. The
stabilization is therefore a numerical safety net rather than a factor
in the reported LJSpeech results. The forward and inverse transforms are

```math
y=(z-\bm{\mu})Q\tilde{\Lambda}^{-1/2}Q^{\top},\qquad z=yQ\tilde{\Lambda}^{1/2}Q^{\top}+\bm{\mu}.
```

Thus, the target coordinate $`x`$ in Section 3.1 is $`x_{s}`$ for the
Scalar system and $`y`$ for the ZCA system. The ZCA Flow is trained and
sampled in $`y`$; $`z`$ is recovered before WaveVAE decoding. The
transform changes neither latent dimension nor frame rate and adds no
learned parameters, making it the fixed coordinate interface shown in
Figure 1. Its statistics are estimated once from valid training frames
and then frozen. Round-trip and codec-invariance audits pass the
pre-specified implementation thresholds: latent round-trip maximum
absolute error below $`10^{-4}`$, waveform mean absolute error below
$`10^{-4}`$, and waveform maximum absolute error below
$`2\times 10^{-3}`$.

ZCA is not optimization-neutral. Let
$`\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}=Q\tilde{\Lambda}Q^{\top}`$ denote the stabilized
covariance used by the transform, whereas $`\bm{\Sigma}`$ is the raw
training covariance reported above. Let
$`\hat{z}=\hat{y}Q\tilde{\Lambda}^{1/2}Q^{\top}+\bm{\mu}`$ be a
prediction mapped back to codec coordinates. Euclidean error in whitened
coordinates is a stabilized Mahalanobis error in codec coordinates:

```math
\|\hat{y}-y\|_{F}^{2}=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(\hat{z}-z)\tilde{\bm{\Sigma}}^{-1}(\hat{z}-z)^{\top}\right].
```

Here the trace sums the per-frame Mahalanobis errors over the $`T`$
acoustic frames. Thus, training in $`y`$ changes the effective loss
metric in codec coordinates, not only the coordinate system. We
therefore interpret our results as the joint effect of the full ZCA
coordinates and their induced optimization metric, not as causal
evidence that reducing the condition number alone is responsible.

### 3.3 Inference conditions

We distinguish three inference paths:

1.  1.  Strict-GT: MFA phones and ground-truth phone durations, isolating
        acoustic generation.
2.  2.  Cached predicted duration: outputs from the frozen duration model,
        computed once, cached, and reused across systems.
3.  3.  Text-only: lexicon/G2P and duration prediction run at inference time
        from raw text; no MFA alignment, phone cache, or duration cache is
        read.

The three-seed primary analysis uses Val399 strict-GT with fixed E100
non-EMA (raw) checkpoints; text-only results evaluate the selected
deployment systems.

For normalized eigenvalues $`p_{i}=\lambda_{i}/\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}`$,
effective rank is $`\exp(-\sum_{i}p_{i}\log p_{i})`$ and participation
ratio is $`(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i})^{2}/\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}^{2}`$. The
within-lexical-phone statistic is not a mean of per-phone condition
numbers. It first forms the frame-weighted pooled within-phone
covariance

```math
\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{within}}=\frac{1}{N_{\mathcal{P}}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}N_{p}\bm{\Sigma}_{p},
```

where $`\mathcal{P}`$ contains lexical phones only, $`N_{p}`$ is the
number of frames assigned to phone $`p`$,
$`N_{\mathcal{P}}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}N_{p}`$, and padding, unknown,
silence, and short-pause tokens are excluded. The reported condition
numbers are computed with a relative eigenvalue floor of $`10^{-6}`$,
before and after applying the global ZCA matrix to
$`\bm{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{within}}`$. This is a diagnostic floor, distinct
from the $`10^{-5}`$ relative floor used to construct the global ZCA
transform in Section 3.2; the smaller diagnostic value avoids
unnecessarily clamping low-variance within-phone directions. No
eigenvalue of the pooled lexical within-phone covariance, either before
or after global ZCA, falls below this diagnostic floor, so the floor is
inactive and the reported conditions reflect the raw pooled covariances.
This audit confirms that ZCA improves conditioning beyond global
between-phone variation; it does not guarantee improved generation
quality.

The base Flow backbone uses the base LR; the condition LR applies only

Mode 1 does not use this continuous-time sampler: it is trained at fixed
$`t=1`$ on the perturbed near-clean inputs described in Section 3.1. The
endpoint-loss time weight is capped at $`t=0.95`$, so samples with
$`t>0.95`$ receive a weight of approximately $`400`$ rather than the

runs, DistributedSampler(drop_last=True) assigns 3,020 examples to each
rank; the local loader then retains 503 complete batches of six and
drops two examples per rank. Each epoch therefore contains 503
synchronized global batches ($`12{,}072`$ examples), drops eight
examples in total, and yields $`50{,}300`$ updates over 100 epochs.

sampling-efficiency sweep below instead reports RTF recorded by the same
evaluation path for all cells; it remains an internal strict-GT
generation-path measurement rather than an independently standardized
latency benchmark.

### 4.4 Inference-time sampling-efficiency audit

We run a zero-retraining step sweep on Val399 strict-GT for all three
matched raw-checkpoint pairs. The Euler endpoint remains
$`t_{\mathrm{end}}=0.95`$, the solver remains Euler, and every cell
retains one terminal mode 1 call. Total calls are 20, 10, 5, 3, and 2,
corresponding to 19, 9, 4, 2, and 1 mode 0 updates, respectively. For
$`K`$ total calls, the mode 0 step size is $`\Delta t=0.95/(K-1)`$, so
every cell reaches the same mode 0 endpoint before refinement. No
checkpoint is retrained or fine-tuned for a particular call count. The
two-call cell is a large-step Euler diagnostic, not a MeanFlow or
interval-average-velocity model.

The timed block begins at the strict-GT synthesize_alignment call. It
includes loading and preparing the cached alignment payload,
phone-conditioning computation, all Flow calls, coordinate restoration,
and frozen WaveVAE decoding. In ZCA cells, the inverse $`64\times 64`$
transform is executed inside the timed decoder path and is therefore
included; one-time checkpoint and covariance-statistics loading is
outside the timer. The measurement excludes text normalization/G2P,
duration prediction, waveform writing, ASR, and UTMOS evaluation. We
therefore call it strict-GT generation-path RTF, use it only for matched
internal quality–latency comparisons, and do not compare it directly
with published end-to-end latency from other systems.

### 4.5 Metrics and uncertainty

Content is evaluated with openai/whisper-large-v3 \[17\] using corpus
WER, mean utterance WER, P90 WER (the 90th percentile of utterance-level
WER), and exact match after the same text normalization used for WER.
Proxy naturalness is evaluated with UTMOS22 Strong (UTMOS hereafter),
implemented by tarepan/SpeechMOS:v1.2.0/utmos22_strong \[18\], after
resampling to 16 kHz. UTMOS22 was developed for VoiceMOS Challenge data
derived mainly from Blizzard Challenge and Voice Conversion Challenge
synthetic speech \[18\]; it was not calibrated to EAF-TTS outputs.
Paired same-utterance differences are more defensible than absolute
scores but remain proxies. We also report an internal DTW-MFCC distance,
which is not directly comparable to public MCEP-based MCD values.

The primary full-step evaluation script computes normal-approximation
intervals, $`\bar{x}\pm 1.96s/\sqrt{n}`$, for system scores and paired
utterance differences, where $`n`$ is the number of evaluated utterances
(399 for Val399), not the number of training seeds. These intervals do
not cluster by text length, phone composition, or speaker. The
sampling-efficiency audit uses 20,000 paired utterance-bootstrap
resamples. For WER and exact match, we additionally report a
fixed-three-seed bootstrap that jointly resamples the same utterance IDs
across all three trained systems; this quantifies uncertainty over the
evaluated texts for these fixed seeds, not over unseen training seeds.
The no-update gradient audit uses a separate paired batch bootstrap with
20,000 resamples. These bootstrap protocols are not pooled. Across
training seeds, we report mean and seed SD only; three seeds are
insufficient for a high-powered seed-level significance test. This is a
retrospective controlled analysis, and the UTMOS endpoint was not
publicly preregistered. WER, exact match, and DTW-MFCC are retained to
reduce selective positive reporting. Future blind-text results should
use a prospectively frozen paired analysis or a mixed-effects model.

## 6 Analysis and Discussion

### 6.1 What the results support

Scalar Flow maps a unit Gaussian to a target with covariance condition
number 1355.44. ZCA equalizes second-order principal-direction scales
and may reduce source–target covariance mismatch or high-variance
domination of Euclidean regression. The three matched-seed effects and
text-only deployment result are consistent with improved conditional
distribution modeling rather than improved codec reconstruction alone.

The evidence does not isolate condition number as the cause. Full ZCA
rotates and rescales coordinates and changes both Flow and
auxiliary-loss metrics. We have not yet completed LJSpeech
partial-whitening, rotation-only, or reference-coordinate-loss
interventions. The supported claim is the aggregate effect of full ZCA
with train-data-estimated statistics.

### 6.2 Naturalness headroom can be exchanged for fewer calls

The sampling sweep adds a practical but distinct finding. It does not
show that ZCA improves the numerical order, stability region, or
low-step accuracy of Euler integration: every robustness interaction is
negative. Scalar itself improves at moderate call reductions, which
suggests that the 20-call rollout may accumulate local
endpoint-prediction errors. Instead, full-step ZCA begins from a
substantially higher UTMOS level. Reducing its calls spends part of this
margin while improving RTF and, at three to five calls, ASR-derived
content and DTW-MFCC point estimates.

This distinction matters for deployment claims. The supported statement
is that _ZCA supplies proxy-naturalness headroom that can be exchanged
for measured inference speed under this evaluator_. It is not evidence
that whitening intrinsically accelerates ODE solving. ZCA-5 is the
balanced operating point; ZCA-3 is the aggressive low-latency point; the
two-call result is a failure boundary. Because the RTF excludes text
normalization/G2P and duration prediction and was not obtained from an
independent latency harness, we do not compare it with published
end-to-end RTF values.

### 6.3 Covariance whitening is not perceptual whitening

We estimate frozen-decoder log-mel sensitivity on 32 Val utterances,
four boundary-safe five-frame windows each (128 windows). Let $`q_{i}`$
and $`\lambda_{i}`$ denote covariance eigenvectors and eigenvalues, and
define

```math
s_{i}=\mathbb{E}_{z}\left[\left\|J_{\operatorname{logmel}\circ D}(z)q_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}\right].
```

A unit direction in ZCA coordinates maps through the inverse transform
to $`\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}q_{i}`$, so its diagonal sensitivity proxy is
$`\lambda_{i}s_{i}`$. We report

```math
\kappa_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{raw}}=\frac{\max_{i}s_{i}}{\min_{i}s_{i}},\qquad\kappa_{\mathrm{diag}}^{\mathrm{ZCA}}=\frac{\max_{i}\lambda_{i}s_{i}}{\min_{i}\lambda_{i}s_{i}}.
```

Spearman correlation between $`\lambda_{i}`$ and $`s_{i}`$ is 0.276. The
raw diagonal proxy is 30.54, the ZCA proxy is 21,740.76, and the ZCA
P95/P05 ratio is 125.84. The much larger ZCA proxy arises after
multiplying $`s_{i}`$ by $`\lambda_{i}`$, whose dynamic range (1355.44)
is far larger than that of $`s_{i}`$ (30.54); their weak rank
correlation means that this covariance scaling need not equalize decoder
sensitivity. In other words, equalizing data variance can preserve or
amplify decoder-metric anisotropy when high-variance directions are also
decoder-sensitive. These are local diagonal proxies in the covariance
eigenbasis, not full spatiotemporal Gram condition numbers. A small
finite-difference check uses a latent-coordinate perturbation of
magnitude $`\epsilon=0.1`$ along unit covariance eigenvectors. It yields
a finite-difference/JVP ratio median of 1.127, with 87.5% of directions
within $`[0.5,2]`$, indicating that JVP-based sensitivity agrees in
scale with finite differences for most checked directions. This supports
scale validation but not the extreme-value condition proxy.

ZCA therefore whitens data covariance but not the decoder-induced
perceptual metric. The audit also does not explain the positive UTMOS
result: proxy anisotropy becomes larger while generated UTMOS improves.

### 6.4 Gradient conflict is not a simple harm indicator

On 32 matched no-update batches, ZCA changes the latent–mel and mel–STFT
gradient cosines by $`-0.102`$ and $`-0.377`$, respectively. The mean
change across three decoder-related pairs is $`-0.159`$, with a
20,000-resample paired batch-bootstrap 95% CI of $`[-0.185,-0.129]`$.
This bootstrap is specific to the gradient audit; the primary full-step
UTMOS intervals use the normal approximation described in Section 4.5.
ZCA thus increases several local multi-objective conflicts even though
final UTMOS improves substantially.

This negative audit shows that stronger local gradient conflict is not
sufficient to predict worse generation. It may reflect a redistribution
of responsibilities among latent, mel, and STFT objectives. We do not
infer that gradient surgery or dynamic loss weighting would be
ineffective.

### 6.5 Proxy naturalness versus content

At the primary 20-call setting, UTMOS improves for all three seeds,
while WER, exact match, and DTW-MFCC are seed dependent. ZCA may
increase the effective weight of low-variance acoustic directions that
affect timbre, harmonics, or local dynamics preferred by the UTMOS
predictor without improving phone boundaries or ASR confidence. The
few-call deployment comparisons show that the ZCA UTMOS margin can be
retained without detected paired content degradation relative to
Scalar-20, but they do not establish that ZCA intrinsically improves
intelligibility. Current results cannot distinguish human-perceived
improvement from predictor preference.

We therefore treat ZCA as a candidate UTMOS proxy-naturalness
preconditioner, not a universal replacement for Scalar coordinates. As
an illustrative post-hoc deployment gate, suppose WER may worsen by at
most 0.05 percentage points, exact match by at most 0.5 points, and
pathological count may not increase. The selected seed-2027 text-only
result fails this gate (WER $`+0.0595`$, exact $`-2.01`$). This
illustrative gate was not preregistered and was not used to select any
reported system; deployment requires a prospectively frozen gate,
matched checkpoint controls, and listening tests.

### 6.6 Engineering properties

ZCA statistics are computed once offline, add no trainable parameters,
and require only a 64-dimensional linear inverse before decoding. The
interface is independent of text and test audio and can be used in
strict-GT, predicted-duration, and text-only paths. It does require
retraining the Flow: a Scalar-trained checkpoint cannot be switched to
ZCA coordinates post hoc. Once trained, however, the same checkpoint can
be evaluated at the three- and five-call operating points without
additional optimization.

## 7 Limitations and Threats to Validity

Scope and external validity. UTMOS22 Strong is an automatic naturalness
proxy, not human MOS. It was not calibrated to EAF-TTS outputs or
publicly preregistered as the primary endpoint, and no human listening
study was conducted. Absolute proxy scores remain below the frozen-codec
reconstruction reference, so the reported gain should not be read as
approaching human naturalness. The study covers one single-speaker
dataset, one WaveVAE checkpoint, one latent dimension, and one EAF-TTS
configuration. We make no cross-speaker, cross-dataset, cross-codec,
multilingual, noisy, expressive, or style-controlled generalization
claim, and we do not compare EAF-TTS end to end with external TTS
systems.

Internal validity and causal attribution. Full ZCA jointly changes the
training and sampling coordinates, the induced codec-space loss metric,
and mode 1 perturbation geometry. The reported result is therefore a
full-protocol effect, not a causal effect of lowering the covariance
condition number alone. We do not report partial whitening,
rotation-only, per-channel scaling, reference-coordinate loss,
inference-only ZCA, or conditional/local whitening controls. Padding is
excluded by the valid-frame mask, but sensitivity to additionally
excluding silence or short pauses is not reported. Mode 1 is trained on
perturbed clean latents but receives mode 0 ODE outputs at inference, so
its training and inference input distributions are not assumed to match.
Its numeric perturbation scale is matched in each active Flow coordinate
rather than in codec space. We did not run codec-space-matched
perturbation, no-mode-1, or mode-0-output-fed mode 1 ablations. The
text-only selected-checkpoint comparison mixes Scalar EMA and ZCA raw
checkpoints; it is confounded by checkpoint type and is treated as
directional deployment evidence, not as a causal coordinate estimate.

Measurement and statistics. Test399 was repeatedly accessed and is not
blind-test evidence. The primary evidence uses text-disjoint Val399, but
Val399 is a development validation set rather than a prospectively
untouched blind test set. Scalar and ZCA deliberately share
hyperparameters, schedule, and checkpoint rule to isolate the coordinate
intervention; neither was tuned separately, so the study does not
establish optimality under per-system tuning. Three seeds are
insufficient for high-powered seed-level inference. Primary full-step
UTMOS intervals use normal approximations without clustering by text
length, phone composition, or speaker; sampling and gradient audits use
separate paired bootstrap protocols rather than one mixed-effects
analysis. At 20 calls, WER, exact-match rate, and DTW-MFCC do not
improve consistently, so the positive effect is localized mainly to
proxy naturalness rather than content fidelity. Few-call paired content
intervals quantify uncertainty over the evaluated texts for three fixed
seeds, not over new training runs; intervals containing zero support “no
statistically detectable degradation,” not equivalence. The internal
DTW-MFCC distance is not directly comparable with public MCEP-based MCD.
Sampling-sweep RTF includes Flow calls, coordinate restoration, and
frozen WaveVAE decoding, but excludes text normalization/G2P, duration
prediction, waveform writing, ASR, and UTMOS. It is not an independently
standardized end-to-end latency benchmark and should not be compared
directly with published end-to-end RTF.

Mechanistic interpretation. Frozen-decoder and gradient audits, together
with inconsistent content/reference-distance results, constrain but do
not establish a mechanism. We do not show that ZCA improves generation
by lowering the condition number, or that frozen-decoder sensitivity
aligns with data covariance. At every reduced call count, ZCA’s
advantage over Scalar is smaller than at 20 calls. The few-call results
therefore demonstrate usable full-step proxy-naturalness headroom, not
intrinsically better low-step integration. A mechanistic account, a
prospectively untouched test set, and broader validation remain open.

## 8 Conclusion

LJSpeech WaveVAE training latents are strongly anisotropic even though
the frozen codec reconstructs real latents well. A fixed ZCA interface
with no learned parameters, whose statistics are estimated only from
training frames, raises Val399 E100 raw UTMOS for all three matched
training seeds, with mean gain $`+0.405\pm 0.027`$. A
selected-checkpoint text-only comparison also gains $`+0.4311`$ without
test-time acoustic caches, although it mixes EMA and raw checkpoints.
Frozen-decoder sensitivity and gradient audits constrain simple
explanations.

The inference-only sweep shows that this proxy-naturalness margin can be
exchanged for fewer model calls. Relative to Scalar-20, ZCA-5 retains a
$`+0.375\pm 0.027`$ UTMOS advantage with no statistically detectable
paired WER or exact-match degradation for the three fixed seeds,
although equivalence is not established, while reducing measured
strict-GT generation-path RTF by $`3.34\times`$. ZCA-3 retains a smaller
$`+0.165\pm 0.031`$ advantage at a $`5.03\times`$ operating point,
whereas two calls cause a severe UTMOS collapse. Because all
reduced-call robustness interactions are negative, the result is not
evidence that ZCA intrinsically improves low-step integration; it
reflects usable naturalness headroom.

The evidence supports a narrow conclusion: within this single-speaker,
single-WaveVAE LJSpeech/EAF-TTS protocol, global ZCA with
train-data-only statistics consistently improves UTMOS automatic proxy
naturalness across three matched seeds and enables favorable measured
few-call operating points. It has not yet been shown to improve human
perception or to provide a general speed advantage across hardware,
datasets, codecs, or TTS architectures. Blind text, listening tests,
matched text-only checkpoints, and coordinate-versus-metric ablations
remain necessary.

## 9 Data, Ethics, and Code Availability

LJSpeech contains 13,100 English utterances from one speaker and is
released in the public domain \[19\]. This study uses public audio and
existing automatic metrics and recruits no new human participants.
Future listening tests will follow applicable ethics review,
informed-consent, and data-retention requirements.

Scalar/ZCA configurations, covariance-statistics code, evaluation
scripts, and result summaries are available from the authors upon
request. A versioned public archive with checkpoint hashes and an
environment lockfile is in preparation.
