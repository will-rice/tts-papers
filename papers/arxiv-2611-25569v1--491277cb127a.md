# UMelCodec: An Ultra-Low-Bitrate and Lightweight Mel-Domain Neural Speech Codec

###### Abstract

Ultra-low-bitrate speech coding is crucial for bandwidth-constrained
communication and compact storage, but preserving intelligibility,
naturalness, and fine-grained acoustic details at bitrates of only a few
hundred bps remains challenging. Existing purely acoustic codecs
generally operate at relatively high bitrates, whereas incorporating
semantic representations can reduce the bitrate, but at the cost of
increased complexity. Therefore, we propose UMelCodec, a purely acoustic
neural speech codec operating in the mel domain at an ultra-low bitrate
of 325 bps with a lightweight architecture. UMelCodec consists of two
stages. In the first mel-domain coding stage, alias-free Snake-ConvNeXt
(AF-SC) encoder–decoder backbones model temporal and harmonic
structures, a factorized single-codebook vector quantizer (VQ) enables
ultra-low-bitrate quantization, and a multi-scale mel-spectrogram
discriminator (MSMD) improves mel-spectrogram reconstruction. In the
second waveform synthesis stage, a lightweight modified discrete cosine
transform (MDCT)-based neural vocoder predicts the MDCT spectrum from
the reconstructed mel spectrogram and recovers the waveform through
inverse MDCT. Experiments on LibriTTS dataset demonstrate that UMelCodec
outperforms strong baselines across most objective reconstruction
metrics while maintaining competitive subjective naturalness. In terms
of model complexity and inference efficiency, UMelCodec has only 19.67 M
parameters, requires 1.93 G MACs, and supports real-time inference on
both GPU and CPU platforms.

## I Introduction

Speech coding aims to represent speech signals with compact bitstreams
and reconstruct intelligible and natural waveforms at decoder side. It
is a key technique for bandwidth-constrained communication, speech
storage, etc.

Traditional speech codecs rely on carefully designed signal-processing
modules. Opus \[1\] and EVS \[2\] provide robust speech quality but
generally operate at bitrates of several kbps or higher. Recent
acoustic-level neural codecs improve rate–distortion performance through
learned signal representations. Among them, waveform-domain codecs,
e.g., SoundStream \[3\], EnCodec \[4\], DAC \[5\], and BigCodec \[6\],
directly model time-domain signals using encoder–quantizer–decoder
architectures, but their deeply stacked downsampling and upsampling
layers incur substantial computational cost. Spectral-domain codecs
reduce the modeling burden by exploiting structured time–frequency
representations: APCodec \[7\], MDCTCodec \[8\], and Spectral
Codec \[9\] quantize amplitude–phase spectral, modified discrete cosine
transform (MDCT) spectral, and mel-spectral representations,
respectively. However, these codecs still operate at relatively high
bitrates, and preserving fine-grained acoustic details in the
few-hundred-bps regime remains challenging. To address this bitrate
limitation at the acoustic level, WavTokenizer \[10\], Mimi \[11\], and
Stable Codec \[12\] achieve low-bitrate coding with relatively complex
architectures, while SNAC \[13\] improves compression efficiency.
Nevertheless, few-hundred-bps coding with low computational complexity
remains challenging. FlowMAC \[14\], MuCodec \[15\], and
FMelCodec \[16\] introduce flow-matching-based refinement \[17\] for
ultra-low-bitrate mel-spectrogram coding, but its iterative mel
refinement and waveform generation require multiple inference steps and
increase computational complexity. Alternatively, semantic-assisted
codecs, e.g., SemantiCodec \[18\] and FocalCodec \[19\], achieve
ultra-low-bitrate coding by leveraging high-level representations from
large pretrained models; however, the additional upstream models
substantially increase the overall model size and computational cost and
may discard fine-grained acoustic information. Therefore, jointly
achieving an ultra-low bitrate, low computational complexity, and high
acoustic fidelity remains a challenge.

To address these limitations, we develop UMelCodec, an Ultra-low-bitrate
and lightweight Mel-domain neural speech Codec that relies solely on
acoustic representations and operates at only 325 bps. The key
contribution lies in jointly designing harmonic-aware mel-domain
compression and efficient waveform synthesis to balance reconstruction
quality, bitrate and complexity. Specifically, the mel-domain coding
stage uses alias-free Snake-ConvNeXt (AF-SC) encoder–decoder backbones,
a factorized single-codebook vector quantizer (VQ), and a multi-scale
mel-spectrogram discriminator (MSMD) to compress and reconstruct mel
spectrograms at an ultra low bitrate. The waveform synthesis stage then
employs a lightweight vocoder to predict MDCT spectra from the
reconstructed mel spectrograms and recover the waveforms. Experiments
show that UMelCodec surpasses strong baselines on most objective
reconstruction metrics while achieving competitive subjective
naturalness. Moreover, UMelCodec maintains low model complexity and
computational cost while supporting real-time inference across different
hardware platforms.

## II Proposed Method

### II-A Overview

Fig. 1: Overall framework of the proposed UMelCodec.

As illustrated in Fig. 1(a), the proposed UMelCodec is a two-stage
ultra-low-bitrate speech coding framework. Let
$`\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}`$ and $`\hat{\bm{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{T}`$ denote
the input and reconstructed waveforms sampled at $`f_{s}`$,
respectively, where $`T`$ is the number of waveform samples. In the
first mel-domain coding stage, a mel spectrogram
$`\bm{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}`$ is first extracted from $`\bm{x}`$
using a hop size of $`H`$ samples, yielding $`N=T/H`$ mel frames, where
$`D`$ is the number of mel bins. The mel-domain codec then maps
$`\bm{M}`$ to a discrete index sequence
$`\bm{d}=[d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{N^{\prime}}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{N}^{N^{\prime}}`$
and reconstructs mel-spectrogram
$`\hat{\bm{M}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}`$, where each
$`d_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,K\}`$, $`K`$ is the codebook size, and
$`N^{\prime}`$ is the number of latent frames after downsampling. In the
second waveform synthesis stage, an MDCT-based vocoder reconstructs
$`\hat{\bm{x}}`$ from $`\hat{\bm{M}}`$.

### II-B Mel-Domain Coding Stage

As shown in Fig. 1(b), the mel codec consists of a mel-analysis encoder,
a factorized single-codebook VQ, and a mel-synthesis decoder. Given an
input mel spectrogram $`\bm{M}`$, the mel-analysis encoder first applies
an input convolution to map each $`D`$-dimensional mel frame into a
higher-dimensional feature space. The resulting sequence is processed by
eight AF-SC blocks to model temporal dependencies and periodic acoustic
structures. A strided convolution then reduces the temporal frame rate
by an overall factor of $`r`$, yielding $`N^{\prime}=N/r`$ latent
frames. Finally, a latent convolution reduces the feature dimension and
produces the latent sequence
$`\bm{Z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N^{\prime}\times C}`$ for quantization, where
$`C`$ denotes the latent feature dimension.

As shown in Fig. 1(c), AF-SC block follows a ConvNeXt-style
architecture \[20, 21\]. A large-kernel depthwise convolution first
models temporal context, followed by layer normalization and a pointwise
projection that expands the feature dimension. An alias-free (AF)
SnakeBeta activation \[22, 23\] introduces a periodic inductive bias for
modeling harmonic structures, after which a second pointwise projection
restores the input dimension. Layer scaling and a residual connection
are further applied to stabilize optimization and preserve input
information. AF-SnakeBeta applies the SnakeBeta nonlinearity at an
increased temporal resolution, with fixed low-pass filters (LPFs) placed
before and after the activation:

```math
\mathrm{AF\text{-}SnakeBeta}(\bm{f})=\mathcal{D}_{a}\!\left(\bm{w}_{\mathrm{down}}*\mathrm{SnakeBeta}\!\left(\bm{w}_{\mathrm{up}}*\mathcal{U}_{a}(\bm{f})\right)\right), \tag{1}
```

where $`\bm{f}`$ denotes the input feature sequence,
$`\mathcal{U}_{a}(\cdot)`$ and $`\mathcal{D}_{a}(\cdot)`$ denote
upsampling and downsampling by a factor of $`a`$, respectively, and
$`*`$ denotes convolution. The fixed Kaiser-windowed sinc LPF kernels
$`\bm{w}_{\mathrm{up}}`$ and $`\bm{w}_{\mathrm{down}}`$ suppress the
spectral images introduced by upsampling and remove the high-frequency
components generated by SnakeBeta before downsampling, respectively,
thereby reducing aliasing.

The latent sequence $`\bm{Z}`$ produced by the mel-analysis encoder is
subsequently discretized using the factorized single-codebook VQ.
Following \[5\], each $`C`$-dimensional latent vector is projected to a
lower-dimensional quantization space of dimension $`C_{q}`$, where
$`C_{q}<C`$, and matched to its nearest entry in a codebook of $`K`$
vectors. The resulting frame-level indices form the discrete sequence
$`\bm{d}`$. The selected codebook vectors are then projected back to
dimension $`C`$ before being passed to the mel-synthesis decoder. This
$`C\!\rightarrow\!C_{q}\!\rightarrow\!C`$ factorization facilitates code
assignment under the single-codebook setting. The resulting theoretical
bitrate is $`R=\frac{f_{s}}{Hr}\log_{2}K`$ (bps).

The mel-synthesis decoder reverses the transformations of the
mel-analysis encoder. A latent convolution first expands the quantized
representation to the decoder feature dimension. A transposed
convolution then upsamples the temporal sequence by a factor of $`r`$,
restoring the original mel frame rate and sequence length $`N`$. The
upsampled features are further refined by eight AF-SC blocks, and an
output convolution maps them back to $`D`$ mel bins to obtain the
reconstructed mel spectrogram $`\hat{\bm{M}}`$.

### II-C Waveform Synthesis Stage

The neural waveform synthesis stage reconstructs the time-domain
waveform from the decoded mel spectrogram using a lightweight MDCT-based
vocoder. First, an amplitude-spectrum estimate is derived from
$`\hat{\bm{M}}`$ using the pseudo-inverse of the mel filter-bank matrix.
The estimated spectrum is then processed by an input projection and
eight ConvNeXt v2 blocks \[21\] to model temporal and spectral
dependencies, followed by an output projection that predicts the
corresponding MDCT spectrum. Finally, the reconstructed waveform
$`\hat{\bm{x}}`$ is obtained through inverse MDCT.

### II-D Training Strategy

The modules in mel-domain coding stage and neural waveform synthesis
stage are trained separately. For the mel-domain codec, the training
objective comprises reconstruction, quantization, and adversarial
losses. The mel-spectrogram reconstruction loss
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}`$ measures the equal-weight $`\ell_{1}`$
and $`\ell_{2}`$ distances between $`\bm{M}`$ and $`\hat{\bm{M}}`$.
Following VQ-VAE \[24\], the quantization objective includes a
commitment loss $`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{com}}`$ and a codebook loss
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cb}}`$. To improve the perceptual and structural
fidelity of the reconstructed mel spectrograms, we employ an MSMD
comprising three sub-discriminators at progressively downsampled
temporal resolutions to distinguish real and reconstructed mel
spectrograms. The resulting adversarial loss
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}`$ and feature-matching loss
$`\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fm}}`$ encourage realistic mel-spectral patterns
and stabilize adversarial training, respectively. The overall objective
is

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{G}= \displaystyle\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{com}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{com}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{cb}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cb}} \tag{2}
```

```math
\displaystyle+\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{adv}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{fm}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fm}},
```

where $`\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}`$, $`\lambda_{\mathrm{com}}`$,
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{cb}}`$, $`\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}`$, and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{fm}}`$ are hyperparameters.

The MDCT-based vocoder is trained using an MDCT-domain multi-scale
discriminator, which processes MDCT spectra at three resolutions through
parallel two-dimensional convolutional branches. Following the
multi-scale adversarial learning strategy \[25, 26\], its objective
combines adversarial and feature-matching losses with MDCT-spectrum and
mel-spectrogram reconstruction losses.

## III Experimental Setups

### III-A Dataset

We conducted experiments on the LibriTTS corpus \[27\], with all speech
clips downsampled to $`f_{s}=`$16 kHz. For training, we used the full
official training set, including train-clean-100, train-clean-360, and
train-other-500. The UMelCodec was evaluated on test-clean and
test-other to assess reconstruction quality under clean and more
challenging acoustic conditions.

### III-B Implementation

For UMelCodec¹¹ 1 Audio samples can be found at
[https://redmist328.github.io/UMelCodecdemo/](https://redmist328.github.io/UMelCodecdemo/).,
mel spectrograms were extracted from waveforms using an FFT size of
1024, a window length of 640, a hop size of $`H=160`$, and $`D=80`$ mel
bins. For the AF-SC blocks in both the mel-analysis encoder and
mel-synthesis decoder, the feature and intermediate dimensions were set
to 256 and 512, respectively, while the resampling factor $`a`$ in the
AF-SnakeBeta activation was set to 2. The temporal
downsampling/upsampling factor was set to $`r=4`$. The latent dimension,
quantization dimension, and codebook size were set to $`C=32`$,
$`C_{q}=8`$, and $`K=8192`$, respectively, corresponding to a
theoretical bitrate of 325 bps. The loss weights
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{rec}}`$, $`\lambda_{\mathrm{com}}`$,
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{cb}}`$, $`\lambda_{\mathrm{adv}}`$, and
$`\lambda_{\mathrm{fm}}`$ were set to 45, 2.5, 10, 0.1, and 0.1,
respectively. AdamW \[28\] optimizer was used for training with a
learning rate of $`2\times 10^{-4}`$, $`\beta_{1}=0.8`$,
$`\beta_{2}=0.99`$, and an exponential decay factor of 0.999. The
separately trained MDCT-based vocoder employed ConvNeXt v2 blocks, each
with feature and intermediate dimensions of 513 and 1536, respectively.
The MDCT spectra were computed using a 320-sample cosine window with a
hop size of 160, yielding 160 frequency bins.

### III-C Baselines

We compared UMelCodec with DAC \[5\], BigCodec \[6\], FMelCodec \[16\],
SemantiCodec \[18\], and FocalCodec \[19\]. Since DAC, BigCodec, and
FMelCodec do not provide official 325-bps checkpoints, we retrained them
on the same dataset using a 25-Hz frame rate and a single 8192-entry
codebook. For SemantiCodec and FocalCodec, we used their official
325-bps checkpoints.

### III-D Evaluation Metrics

We evaluated speech reconstruction quality using both objective and
subjective metrics. The objective metrics included the virtual speech
quality objective listener (ViSQOL) \[29\], log-spectral distance (LSD),
mel-cepstral distortion (MCD) \[30\], and short-time objective
intelligibility (STOI) \[31\]. For subjective evaluation, mean opinion
score (MOS) tests were conducted on Amazon Mechanical Turk platform,
where each batch contained 20 utterances rated by at least 25 native
English listeners on a five-point naturalness scale with 0.5-point
increments. Model complexity and inference efficiency were evaluated on
the LibriTTS test-other set using the number of parameters (Param.),
multiply–accumulate operations (MACs), and real-time factor (RTF). MACs
were calculated using a 1-second speech input, while GPU and CPU
real-time factors (RTFs) were measured on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 and
an Intel Xeon E5-2680 v3 processor, respectively.

## IV Experimental Results

### IV-A Comparison with Baseline Codecs

Table I compared UMelCodec with representative acoustic-level codecs,
including waveform- and mel-domain methods, as well as semantic-assisted
codecs, all operating at 325 bps. UMelCodec achieved the best ViSQOL,
MCD, and STOI results on both test sets, together with the best LSD on
test-clean and the joint-best LSD on test-other. Compared with the
waveform-domain DAC and BigCodec, UMelCodec provided higher perceptual
quality, intelligibility, and spectral fidelity, demonstrating the
effectiveness of speech coding based on compact mel-spectral
representations. It also outperformed the generative FMelCodec without
requiring iterative flow-matching refinement. As a purely acoustic
codec, UMelCodec achieved higher full-reference reconstruction fidelity
than SemantiCodec and FocalCodec without relying on large pretrained
representation models, suggesting that their semantic representations
may prioritize linguistic content over fine-grained acoustic details.

The subjective results further supported the objective findings to some
extent. On test-clean, UMelCodec achieved a MOS of 3.59$`\pm`$0.08,
close to the 3.63$`\pm`$0.08 obtained by FocalCodec, while their 95%
confidence intervals overlap. On test-other, FocalCodec obtained a
higher MOS despite its lower objective performance. This may be because
its pretrained semantic representations suppress part of the background
interference and generate cleaner speech rather than strictly preserving
the original acoustic environment. In contrast, UMelCodec maintained
competitive subjective naturalness while more faithfully preserving the
input acoustic characteristics.

In terms of model complexity and inference efficiency, UMelCodec
required only 19.67 M parameters and 1.93 G MACs, both the lowest among
the evaluated codecs. According to the RTF measurements, it achieved
inference speeds of 156.37 times real time on GPU and 8.36 times real
time on CPU, demonstrating efficient deployment on both platforms.

### IV-B Ablation Studies

We then conducted ablation experiments to investigate the contributions
of the main components in UMelCodec, as reported in Table II. UMelCodec
w/o MSMD exhibited lower ViSQOL and higher LSD and MCD than the full
model, while achieving the same STOI. This result suggests that
multi-scale adversarial supervision contributes to the perceptual
quality and spectral fidelity of the reconstructed mel spectrograms by
encouraging realistic temporal structures at different resolutions.
UMelCodec w/o AF filtering, which removed the upsampling, downsampling,
and LPF operations from the AF-SnakeBeta activation, performed slightly
worse than the full model across all four objective metrics. This
suggests that suppressing aliasing around the periodic nonlinearity
benefited perceptual quality, spectral reconstruction, and speech
intelligibility to some extent. UMelCodec w/o VQ factorization also
exhibited consistent degradation across all metrics, particularly in
ViSQOL, MCD, and STOI, indicating that low-dimensional code assignment
facilitated effective quantization and preserved perceptually important
information under the single-codebook setting.

### IV-C Analysis of the Effect of Codebook Size

The codebook size determined both the representation capacity and the
utilization efficiency of the factorized single-codebook VQ, which is
the percentage of codebook selected at least once over the entire test
set. We therefore analyzed its effect to justify the choice of 8192
entries and examine the trade-off between reconstruction quality and
codebook utilization. As shown in Fig. 2, ViSQOL generally improved as
the codebook size increased and reached its maximum at 8192, where all
codebook entries were utilized. Further increasing the codebook size to
16384 reduced the utilization to approximately 70% and slightly degraded
ViSQOL. In this case, although the nominal codebook size was doubled,
the number of utilized entries increased by only about 40% compared with
the fully utilized 8192-entry codebook, indicating that many additional
entries were redundant or rarely selected. Therefore, enlarging the
codebook beyond 8192 provided limited effective capacity while making
quantization less efficient and degrading reconstruction quality. Based
on this trade-off, a codebook size of 8192 was adopted in UMelCodec.

## V Conclusion

This paper presented UMelCodec, an ultra-low-bitrate and lightweight
mel-domain neural speech codec operating at 325 bps without relying on
auxiliary pretrained semantic models or generative models. UMelCodec
employs AF-SC encoder–decoder backbones, a factorized single-codebook
VQ, and an MSMD for mel-spectrogram compression and reconstruction. A
lightweight neural vocoder predicts the MDCT spectrum from
mel-spectrograms and recovers the waveform through inverse MDCT.
Experiments on LibriTTS show that UMelCodec outperforms strong baselines
in objective reconstruction quality while maintaining competitive
subjective naturalness. In addition, UMelCodec requires only 19.67 M
parameters and 1.93 G MACs and supports real-time GPU and CPU inference.
Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of MSMD, alias-free
activation, and VQ factorization, while codebook analysis favors 8192
entries for balancing reconstruction quality and codebook utilization.
