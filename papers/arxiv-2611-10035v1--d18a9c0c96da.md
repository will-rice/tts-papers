# Ninada: Sub-Clause Split-Graph VITS Streaming and Capability-Routed 24 kHz Speech Synthesis Across Twelve Scripts

Hemanth HM Affiliation: [https://h3manth.com](https://h3manth.com)   
$`\cdot`$   
[https://github.com/hemanth/ninada](https://github.com/hemanth/ninada)

###### Abstract

End-to-end VITS text-to-speech models synthesize natural speech in a
single forward pass, yet standard ONNX exports execute monolithically
over an entire clause, delaying Time-To-First-Audio (TTFA) to several
seconds and spiking activation memory. Client-side deployment across
Indic scripts faces four further bottlenecks: 138.8 MB float32
checkpoints per language strain browser caches, mobile WebGPU stalls for
6–14 s compiling convolution shaders while still running slower than
real time ($`1.44\times`$ RTF), small WebAssembly pthread pools stall at
barriers on big.LITTLE mobile CPUs, and mobile Web Audio pipelines drop
frames or mute output under AudioWorklet contention and iOS WKWebView
rules.

We present Ninada, an open-source 24 kHz TTS system covering English,
Classical Sanskrit, and ten Indian languages (sa, en, hi, kn, mr, bn,
ta, te, ml, gu, pa, or) across a capability-routed 48-checkpoint fleet:
24 studio HiFi-GAN v1 specialists (27.9 MB W8A32+FP16 ONNX) for
desktop/WebGPU and 24 distilled c8c8_384 iSTFTNet specialists (21.7 MB
ONNX, $`2.64\times`$ fewer vocoder MACs via an exact NOLA dual-window
ConvTranspose1d inverse STFT with zero complex ops) for mobile WASM,
plus a 0.0 MB zero-dependency Klatt fallback. At runtime, Ninada splits
a single ONNX file in memory in $`<15`$ ms into prior, flow, and
windowed vocoder sub-sessions streaming 12-frame halos with
raised-cosine crossfades. On $`N=480`$ held-out 24 kHz studio
utterances, Ninada achieves 3.22 dB 0–8 kHz MCD (3.09 dB mobile
iSTFTNet; 1.33 dB 24-voice distillation MCD, vs. 5.17 dB for the
1,526.8 MB 16 kHz Meta MMS fleet) and 6.07 dB 8–12 kHz error, delivering
89–155 ms audible startup on desktop WebGPU and 538–649 ms zero-underrun
streaming at 0.75–0.76$`\times`$ RTF on a physical Google Pixel 10 Pro
XL.

## 1 Introduction

Running speech synthesis directly inside a client browser or local edge
process eliminates server inference costs, network tail latency, and
transcript transmission to external APIs. Screen readers,
language-learning tools, bilingual voice interfaces, and offline
educational readers across South Asian languages require full-band
24 kHz audio, native script fidelity across Brahmic and Latin
orthographies, sub-second audible startup, and uninterrupted playback on
everyday phones.

Variational Inference with adversarial learning for end-to-end
Text-to-Speech (VITS) \[4\] suits client deployment because it avoids
autoregressive mel decoding. However, standard VITS exports hit four
systems bottlenecks in web browsers:

1.  1.  Utterance-Blocking Graph Execution: Standard VITS ONNX exports chain
        the text encoder, stochastic duration predictor, normalizing flow,
        and HiFi-GAN generator \[6\] into one static graph. No audio sample
        is emitted until the entire clause latent
        $`z\in\mathbb{R}^{192\times T}`$ is upsampled by $`256\times`$,
        pushing TTFA past 2–4 s on long clauses and allocating peak
        activations proportional to $`256T`$.
2.  2.  Footprint vs. Orthographic Fidelity: Open checkpoints such as Meta
        MMS-TTS \[9\] generate 16 kHz audio (rolling off above 8 kHz) at
        138.8 MB per language (1,526.8 MB across 11 Indic models).
        Conversely, pooling scripts into a shared Romanized model introduces
        cross-language accent bleed and erases script-specific contrasts
        such as Sanskrit vocalic sonorants, Vedic pitch marks, Malayalam
        atomic chillus, and Tamil/Kannada retroflex sonorants.
3.  3.  Mobile GPU Stalls and Compute Limits: While desktop WebGPU runs
        HiFi-GAN v1 at $`\sim`$0.09$`\times`$ Real-Time Factor (RTF),
        profiling pure-FP16 NHWC WebGPU on a physical Google Pixel 10 Pro XL
        shows 5.9–14.0 s cold shader compilation followed by
        $`1.44\text{--}1.53\times`$ warm effective RTF
        ($`1.90\text{--}2.11\times`$ NCHW), slower than real time.
        Meanwhile, full 28.82 GMAC/s HiFi-GAN v1 also exceeds real time on
        mobile ARM WebAssembly ($`1.30\times`$ RTF at 6 threads).
4.  4.  Asymmetric CPU Barriers and Mobile Audio Quirks: On physical 8-core
        big.LITTLE phones, small WebAssembly pthread pools stall at
        per-operator barriers when threads land on efficiency cores
        ($`2\text{T}=3.60\times`$ and $`3\text{T}=2.40\times`$ RTF vs.
        $`1\text{T}=1.55\times`$ and $`6\text{T}=1.30\times`$).
        Simultaneously, 2.67 ms JavaScript AudioWorklet callbacks contend
        with WASM threads on Android, while iOS WKWebView browsers
        (CriOS/Safari) enforce pthread memory ceilings and hardware
        mute-switch silencing.

raised-cosine crossfades feed integer-frame 48 kHz C++
AudioBufferSourceNode queues on mobile WASM and AudioWorklet ring
buffers on desktop WebGPU.

To resolve these bottlenecks without shipping multi-file voice bundles
or custom ONNX Runtime builds, we present Ninada (Figures 1 and 2). This
paper makes three contributions:

- $`\bullet`$
  Binary ONNX Graph Surgery and NOLA ConvTranspose1d iSTFTNet: We
  introduce a zero-dependency protobuf splitter that partitions a single
  W8A32+FP16 VITS checkpoint at runtime into $`G_{\text{prior}}`$,
  $`G_{\text{flow}}`$, and $`G_{\text{voc}}`$ in $`<15`$ ms, alongside
  an ONNX-native c8c8_384 iSTFTNet \[3\] mobile vocoder that formulates
  exact inverse STFT as a static real-valued ConvTranspose1d filterbank
  ($`10.91`$ GMAC/s, $`>138`$ dB reconstruction SNR, zero complex ops)
  while preserving identical graph-cut signatures.
- $`\bullet`$
  Stability Bound, big.LITTLE Threading, and Mobile Audio Pipeline: We
  derive the minimum valid window size $`V>2Hr_{0}/(1-r_{0})`$ for
  monotonic buffer growth under halo overhead, motivating wide-pool
  ($`6`$-thread) vs. $`1`$-thread big.LITTLE WASM scheduling,
  sentence-gated clause admission when $`r_{0}\geq 1`$, 48 kHz C1
  Hermite AudioBufferSourceNode queuing, and synchronous iOS
  AVAudioSession playback unlock.
- $`\bullet`$
  48-Checkpoint 12-Language Fleet and Dual-Tier Fallback: We train 24
  studio HiFi-GAN specialists (27.9 MB) and distill 24 matching mobile
  iSTFTNet specialists (21.7 MB, $`6.4\times`$ smaller than MMS-TTS) on
  clean CC-BY-4.0 24 kHz studio corpora alongside a 0.0 MB
  Paninian/Indic Klatt-Akshara fallback \[5\], evaluating acoustic
  quality ($`N=480`$) and physical-device streaming telemetry.

## 2 Native Orthography and Capability-Routed Architecture

Ninada exposes a unified streaming API across browser ES modules,
Node.js, and Python (Figure 2). Rather than routing Indic scripts
through lossy Romanization, Tier-1 neural specialists tokenize native
Unicode codepoints directly (sa 144 tokens, en 102, hi 120, kn 85, mr
144, bn 100, ta 61, te 110, ml 101, gu 124, pa 85, or 94), interleaving
blank tokens at even indices to relax monotonic alignment constraints.

### 2.1 Paninian and Pan-Indic Phonological Front-End

For IAST Latin transliteration, prosodic inspection, and Tier-2 formant
synthesis, Ninada runs a deterministic rule-based Grapheme-to-Phoneme
(G2P) and syllable (_akshara_) analyzer in $`<1`$ ms:

- $`\bullet`$
  Classical Sanskrit: Preserves vocalic sonorants
  ($`\text{\@text@daccent{r}},\bar{\text{\@text@daccent{r}}},\text{\@text@daccent{l}}`$),
  resolves homorganic nasal sandhi before stops without collapsing to
  generic anusvāra, absorbs _pūrvarūpa_ avagraha (’) without vowel
  insertion, and extracts Vedic _udātta_/_svarita_ pitch marks into
  prosody metadata while stripping bare combining marks from token
  inputs.
- $`\bullet`$
  Modern Indo-Aryan: Applies context-sensitive Hindi/Marathi medial
  VC_CV and word-final schwa deletion while protecting initial
  syllables, conjunct clusters, and Sanskrit _tatsama_ suffixes \[8\];
  expands Gurmukhi _tippi_/_addak_ gemination and localized numerals.
- $`\bullet`$
  Dravidian Scripts: Preserves short/long mid-vowel contrasts
  ($`e/\bar{e},o/\bar{o}`$), retroflex approximants and laterals (Tamil
  zha, Kannada/Malayalam/Odia retroflex $`l`$), and normalizes Malayalam
  ZWJ sequences into atomic chillu codas.

### 2.2 Capability-Routed Studio and Mobile Specialists

Each Tier-1 voice shares a 6-layer relative-position Transformer text
encoder \[10\], a rational-quadratic neural spline Stochastic Duration
Predictor (SDP) \[2\], and a 4-layer residual affine coupling flow
operating at frame hop $`h=256`$ samples (10.67 ms at
$`f_{s}=24{,}000`$ Hz). The 11 Indic languages are trained on
single-speaker 24 kHz anechoic studio recordings from
ai4bharat/Rasa \[11\] (CC-BY-4.0), while English male and female
specialists are trained on studio condenser recordings from
MikhailT/hifi-tts \[1\] (CC-BY-4.0, resampled cleanly from 44.1 kHz to
24 kHz). Isolating one native speaker and script per checkpoint
eliminates cross-language prosodic bleed.

Because desktop GPUs and mobile CPUs differ by an order of magnitude in
convolution throughput, Ninada pairs each frozen prior and coupling flow
with two capability-routed neural vocoders:

- $`\bullet`$
  Studio Tier (onnx/\*.onnx, 27.9 MB): Desktop WebGPU, desktop
  multi-core WASM, Node.js, and Python load a full time-domain
  HiFi-GAN v1 decoder \[6\] with upsampling rates $`[8,8,2,2]`$ and
  initial width $`512`$ ($`28.82`$ GMAC/s per synthesized second).
- $`\bullet`$
  Mobile Tier (onnx*mobile/\*.onnx, 21.7 MB): Mobile browsers and
  low-RAM hosts ($`\leq 4`$ GB) load a distilled c8c8_384 iSTFTNet
  decoder \[3\] that retains only the first two neural upsampling stages
  $`[8,8]`$ at width $`384`$ ($`64\times`$ neural upsampling to 6 kHz
  frame rate) and delegates the final $`4\times`$ upsampling to an
  inverse STFT head ($`N*{\text{fft}}=16,h\_{\text{stft}}=4`$, $`K=9`$
frequency bins), cutting vocoder compute by $`2.64\times`$ to
$`10.91`$ GMAC/s.

Standard ONNX InverseSTFT and complex-tensor operators lack portable
WebAssembly SIMD kernels in onnxruntime-web. Observing that a periodic
Hann analysis window $`w[n]`$ with $`N_{\text{fft}}=4h_{\text{stft}}`$
satisfies the constant-overlap-add identity
$`\sum_{m}w^{2}[n-mh_{\text{stft}}]\equiv\frac{3}{2}`$ for all $`n`$, we
define the exact Nonzero-Overlap-Add (NOLA) dual window
$`\tilde{w}[n]=\frac{2}{3}w[n]`$ and express inverse STFT synthesis from
predicted log-magnitude $`\hat{M}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times 64T}`$ and phase
$`\hat{\Phi}\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times 64T}`$ as a single static real-valued
transposed convolution:

```math
\hat{y}=\operatorname{ConvT1d}_{h_{\text{stft}}}\!\left(\begin{bmatrix}e^{\hat{M}}\cos\hat{\Phi}\\
e^{\hat{M}}\sin\hat{\Phi}\end{bmatrix},\,\mathbf{B}\right) \tag{1}
```

where $`\operatorname{ConvT1d}_{h_{\text{stft}}}`$ has stride
$`h_{\text{stft}}=4`$ and padding
$`(N_{\text{fft}}-h_{\text{stft}})/2=6`$, and
$`\mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K\times 1\times N_{\text{fft}}}`$ stacks
fixed cosine and sine Fourier basis filters scaled by $`\tilde{w}[n]`$
($`1/N_{\text{fft}}`$ at DC/Nyquist and $`2/N_{\text{fft}}`$ on interior
bins). Equation 1 achieves $`>138`$ dB reconstruction SNR against
torch.istft with zero runtime divisions, complex types, or custom
operators. Each mobile vocoder is distilled for 30,000 steps on paired
teacher latents $`(z,y_{\text{teacher}})`$ and studio posterior latents
$`(z_{\text{post}},y_{\text{studio}})`$ using multi-resolution STFT,
sub-band magnitude, mel-$`L_{1}`$, and Multi-Period Discriminator
feature-matching losses, then surgically grafted into the frozen
specialist graph while preserving the entry conv_pre tensor name.

Mixed W8A32+FP16 compression quantizes encoder and flow projections to
dynamic INT8 (FP32 accumulation) while storing vocoder convolutions and
Fourier basis weights in IEEE FP16, shrinking Studio checkpoints from
112.0 MB to 27.92 MB and Mobile checkpoints to 21.68 MB ($`6.4\times`$
smaller than MMS-TTS). Specialists persist in CacheStorage with an
IndexedDB LRU fallback ($`0`$ network refetches on reload), and
automatically fall back offline to Tier 2: a 0.0 MB cascade Klatt
synthesizer \[5\] with $`-58`$ dBFS inter-clause room tone.

## 3 Sub-Clause Split-Graph Streaming

### 3.1 Runtime Binary ONNX Graph Partitioning

Shipping three separate ONNX files across 48 neural checkpoints would
triple asset management and cache transactions. Instead, Ninada ships
one self-contained .onnx file per voice tier and performs binary
protobuf surgery in JavaScript or Python during session creation
(Figure 1).

Given a serialized ModelProto buffer, the splitter scans top-level
GraphProto wire tags without external protobuf libraries, indexing
NodeProto producers, Initializer byte spans, and ValueInfoProto
descriptors. It locates two topological cut boundaries shared
identically by both HiFi-GAN and iSTFTNet checkpoints:

1.  1.  Prior latent tensor $`z_{p}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 192\times T}`$ and
        mask $`m_{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 1\times T}`$ emitted after SDP
        duration expansion;
2.  2.  Inverted flow latent tensor $`z\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 192\times T}`$
        feeding the entry $`1\times 1`$ conv_pre layer of the waveform
        decoder.

Backward reachability partitions nodes and raw initializer slices into
three valid ModelProto buffers
($`G_{\text{prior}},G_{\text{flow}},G_{\text{voc}}`$) in $`<15`$ ms and
immediately releases the monolithic buffer so worker RAM remains bounded
to a single model footprint.

### 3.2 Receptive-Field Halos and Seam Continuity

Slicing $`z`$ into disjoint frame blocks causes boundary clicks because
dilated residual stacks and transposed convolutions span a finite
temporal receptive field. For window $`k`$ covering valid frames
$`[f_{k},f_{k+1})`$ of length $`V_{k}=f_{k+1}-f_{k}`$, Ninada evaluates
an extended latent slice $`[f_{k}-L_{k},f_{k+1}+R_{k})`$ padded with
symmetric context halos:

```math
L_{k}=\min(f_{k},H),\qquad R_{k}=\min(T-f_{k+1},H) \tag{2}
```

with $`H=12`$ latent frames ($`3{,}072`$ samples, or 128 ms per side),
while $`G_{\text{flow}}`$ runs in bounded blocks with a
$`H_{\text{flow}}=24`$-frame halo. After $`G_{\text{voc}}`$ emits
$`(V_{k}+L_{k}+R_{k})h`$ samples, we trim the $`L_{k}h`$ left-halo
samples, retain $`N_{\text{xf}}=512`$ samples (21.3 ms) from the right
halo as overlap tail $`\tau^{(k)}`$, and blend window $`k+1`$ via a C1
raised-cosine crossfade:

```math
y^{(k+1)}[n]=(1-\alpha_{n})\,\tau^{(k)}[n]+\alpha_{n}\,\hat{y}^{(k+1)}[L_{k+1}h+n] \tag{3}
```

for $`0\leq n<N_{\text{xf}}`$, where
$`\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\!\left(1-\cos\!\frac{\pi(n+0.5)}{N_{\text{xf}}}\right)`$,
followed by a first-order DC blocker ($`R=0.995`$).

### 3.3 Stability Bound, big.LITTLE Scaling, and Mobile Audio

On desktop WebGPU, $`G_{\text{voc}}`$ uses two static frame buckets
($`44`$ frames on Chunk 0 and $`112`$ steady-state) so WGSL shaders
compile only twice at $`\sim`$0.09$`\times`$ RTF. On mobile browsers,
WebGPU is bypassed because shader compilation stalls startup for
5.9–14.0 s while warm 1D transposed convolutions still run at
$`1.44\text{--}1.53\times`$ RTF on phone GPUs; ARM Neon WASM SIMD
compiles in $`<90`$ ms and executes exact unpadded window lengths
$`W_{k}=V_{k}+L_{k}+R_{k}`$.

Let $`r_{0}`$ denote the raw per-frame compute ratio on WASM (wall time
to flow-invert and vocode one latent frame divided by 10.67 ms). An
interior window with $`V`$ valid frames evaluates $`V+2H`$ frames
($`2H=24`$). Playback cushion grows monotonically across interior
windows ($`\Delta B>0`$) if and only if:

```math
(V+2H)r_{0}<V\iff r_{0}<1\;\;\text{and}\;\;V>\frac{2Hr_{0}}{1-r_{0}} \tag{4}
```

Equation 4 governs both model architecture and scheduler design. First,
when $`r_{0}\geq 1`$—as occurs with full HiFi-GAN v1 on physical phone
CPUs ($`r_{0}\approx 1.08`$, effective RTF $`1.30\times`$)—no finite
window $`V`$ can prevent mid-clause buffer depletion; distilling
$`G_{\text{voc}}`$ to c8c8*384 iSTFTNet lowers raw mobile WASM compute
to $`r*{0}\approx 0.56`$, restoring a valid steady-state regime
($`V>30.5`$ frames). Second, even when $`r\_{0}<1`$, overly narrow
windows ($`V=20\Rightarrow 55\%`$ halo overhead) invert effective RTF
above $`1.0\times`$, whereas overly wide initial windows inflate TTFA.

Ninada combines four mechanisms for cross-browser mobile streaming:

1.  1.  Pre-Computed Flow + Window Ramp: Clause 0 vocodes $`W_{0}=68`$
        frames ($`V_{0}=56`$, 597 ms audio) while pre-computing $`128`$
        frames of $`G_{\text{flow}}`$. Chunk 1 ($`V_{1}=60,W_{1}=84`$)
        executes zero flow ops, followed by $`V_{2}=80`$ and steady-state
        $`V_{\geq 3}=96`$ ($`W=120`$, $`20\%`$ halo overhead).
2.  2.  Bimodal big.LITTLE Thread Policy: Homogeneous desktop CPU throttling
        masks mobile barrier contention. On a physical 8-core Pixel 10 Pro
        XL, 2–3 WASM threads run $`1.5\text{--}2.3\times`$ _slower_ than 1
        thread because Emscripten pthreads block on efficiency cores at
        every convolution barrier, whereas a 6-thread pool saturates
        prime/mid cores. Under Cross-Origin Isolation, Ninada therefore
        assigns $`\min(6,C-2)`$ threads when logical cores $`C\geq 8`$ and
        $`1`$ thread when $`C<8`$; on iOS/iPadOS WKWebView, it skips COI
        Service Workers and pins $`1`$ thread to avoid pthread Jetsam
        termination.
3.  3.  Sentence-Gated Clause Admission: On single-threaded or thermally
        throttled devices where observed runtime RTF $`\hat{r}>1`$, Ninada
        admits subsequent clauses into hardware playback once queued lead
        $`B_{\text{ahead}}`$ plus synthesized clause audio
        $`B_{\text{queued}}`$ satisfy
        $`B_{\text{ahead}}+B_{\text{queued}}\geq D_{\text{rem}}\max(0,\hat{r}-1)+W_{\text{next}}`$,
        shifting any unavoidable compute wait cleanly onto natural
        inter-sentence pauses with zero mid-word underruns.
4.  4.  Hardware-Rate C++ Queue and iOS Session Unlock: Mobile browsers
        upsample each 24 kHz chunk on arrival ($`<0.2`$ ms) to hardware rate
        (48 kHz) via a stateful 4-point C1 Hermite interpolator and schedule
        integer-frame AudioBufferSourceNode buffers on Blink/WebKit’s native
        C++ audio thread. On iOS Safari and Chrome (CriOS), Ninada unlocks
        AudioContext synchronously inside touchend/click via a 1-frame
        buffer and activates navigator.audioSession.type = ’playback’
        alongside a silent WAV bridge so speech plays even when the hardware
        Ring/Silent switch is muted.

## 5 Discussion & Limitations

#### Single-Threaded and Un-Isolated Mobile Browsers.

Multi-threaded WebAssembly requires SharedArrayBuffer via Cross-Origin
Isolation (COOP/COEP headers or a Service Worker). On iOS/iPadOS
WKWebView browsers (Safari and Chrome CriOS) where Service Worker
pthread pools risk Jetsam memory kills, and in embedded social-app
WebViews blocking Service Workers, ONNX Runtime executes on 1 WASM
thread. Even with c8c8*384 iSTFTNet lowering 1-thread mobile RTF near
$`0.95\text{--}1.15\times`$, thermal throttling can push
$`r*{0}\geq 1`$; Ninada’s sentence-gated clause admission prevents
mid-word clicks or dropouts by absorbing any deficit cleanly into
natural inter-sentence pauses.

#### Single-Style Studio Voices and Asset Footprint.

Each specialist models one anechoic male or female studio voice per
language without zero-shot speaker cloning, trading multi-speaker
conditioning for compact 21.7–27.9 MB client caching across 24 voices
(48 capability-routed files on Hugging Face, with zero simultaneous
download of both tiers on a single device).

## 6 Related Work

#### End-to-End Indic TTS and Fast Neural Vocoders.

VITS \[4\] unifies variational alignment, normalizing flows \[2\], and
HiFi-GAN \[6\]. Meta MMS \[9\] scaled 16 kHz VITS to 1,100+ languages at
138.8 MB per checkpoint, while AI4Bharat Rasa \[11\] established
studio-grade 24 kHz Indic corpora. Kaneko et al. \[3\] introduced
iSTFTNet to replace outer HiFi-GAN upsamplers with inverse STFT. Ninada
adapts iSTFTNet inside VITS via an exact NOLA dual-window
ConvTranspose1d synthesis filterbank compatible with standard
WebAssembly SIMD and surgical runtime ONNX graph splitting.

#### Client-Side Speech Synthesis.

Formant synthesizers derived from Klatt \[5\] and eSpeak run in
kilobytes with millisecond latency but high spectral distortion
($`>10`$ dB MCD). Browser neural ports such as Piper and Sherpa-ONNX
synthesize full clauses monolithically per worker call. Ninada unifies
sub-clause split-graph neural streaming with capability-routed
desktop/mobile vocoders and a zero-dependency Klatt-Akshara fallback.

## 7 Conclusion

We presented Ninada, a 24,000 Hz streaming text-to-speech system
spanning 12 Indic and English scripts across browser, Node.js, and
Python runtimes. By combining $`<15`$ ms binary ONNX graph surgery,
capability-routed 27.9 MB HiFi-GAN and 21.7 MB NOLA-ConvTranspose1d
iSTFTNet specialists, and receptive-field window sizing paired with C++
AudioBufferSourceNode scheduling, Ninada achieves 3.09–3.22 dB 0–8 kHz
MCD across 480 held-out studio utterances, 89–155 ms audible startup on
desktop WebGPU, and 538–649 ms zero-underrun streaming on physical
smartphone silicon.
