# LoCA: Local Credit Assignment for Reinforcement Learning of Autoregressive Flow-Matching TTS

Jie Liu^(\*)   Junyu Zhou   Junhao Zhong   WeiSen Tang
Affiliation: Rukai Cai   Haoyue Zhan   Ruiqi Xing   Ling Long
Affiliation: Lingguang Zhaxian Technology, Shanghai, China

###### Abstract

Reinforcement learning can improve text-to-speech synthesis through
feedback on generated speech. However, sentence-level rewards do not
identify local errors, while signed regression updates can lead to
excessive fitting or repulsion of sampled outputs. We propose LoCA
(Local Credit Assignment) for autoregressive flow-matching
text-to-speech. LoCA uses ASR-based text alignment to locate content
errors in generated speech and redistribute sentence-level advantages
across acoustic frames. To improve training stability, we introduce
reference-relative positive and negative thresholds that limit continued
fitting and repulsion. Experiments on VoxCPM2 show that LoCA reduces
sample-weighted content error on a multilingual CV3 evaluation subset by
20.5% and 14.3% relative to the pretrained backbone and GROW,
respectively, while improving speaker similarity. LoCA also improves
content accuracy on the Seed-TTS-Eval hard subset. Ablation results
support the contributions of local credit and threshold-based regression
control.

## 1 Introduction

Speech synthesis still produces mispronunciations, omissions and
repetitions, motivating post-training with generated-speech feedback
(Anastassiou et al., 2024; Cui et al., 2025). Continuous-latent models
such as DiTAR (Jia et al., 2025) and the VoxCPM family (Zhou et al.,
2025; Zhou et al., 2026) combine autoregressive context with diffusion
or flow-based acoustics; their regression objectives provide an
interface for reward-driven optimization.

This setting raises two questions. First, where should a sentence reward
act? A single mispronounced word or silent interval may account for an
utterance’s low score, while broadcasting one coefficient treats that
span and its surrounding speech identically. Second, when should a
regression update stop? Positive advantages encourage fitting sampled
trajectories, whereas negative advantages encourage increasing their
regression error. A reference anchor opposes drift but does not specify
separate stopping conditions for fitting and repulsion.

We propose LoCA (Local Credit Assignment) to address both questions.
LoCA centers available frame labels within each utterance and adds them
to its sentence advantage. This redistributes learning coefficients
while preserving their utterance mean. For the second, we introduce
reference-relative positive and negative thresholds. An improvement
budget limits continued fitting, an upper error threshold stops
continued repulsion, and the reference penalty remains active. Together,
these components enable LoCA to target higher synthesis accuracy and
more stable training without changing inference.

Evaluation should also distinguish average accuracy from severe
failures. Mean CER/WER alone does not reveal how often synthesis
produces silence, major content errors or truncated outputs. We define
an automatic severe failure rate (SFR) using explicit content-error,
silence and generation-failure conditions. An utterance satisfying any
condition is counted once; SFR is the proportion of flagged utterances
in the full evaluation set. We use SFR alongside CER/WER as a core
metric for comparing synthesis performance across methods and training
stages.

Our contributions are local credit for targeted content learning and
two-sided regression control for quality improvement and reduced drift.
Baseline comparisons and component ablations on VoxCPM2 examine both
components using hard-set trajectories, selected multilingual CV3 splits
and standard Seed evaluation.

## 2 Related Work

Speech post-training includes multi-reward optimization in Seed-TTS
(Anastassiou et al., 2024), GRPO in GLM-TTS (Cui et al., 2025),
differentiable reward optimization in DiffRO (Gao et al., 2025), and
token-level preference learning in TKTO (Kotoge and Sasaki, 2026). These
methods motivate targeted learning from synthesis feedback; our setting
uses continuous acoustic regression rather than discrete-token policy
updates.

GROW (Yang et al., 2026) directly weights flow-matching regression with
group-relative sentence advantages and anchors velocities to a frozen
reference. FlowAWR (Fu et al., 2026), evaluated in image generation,
derives an advantage-rectified velocity target from a KL-constrained
reward objective; AdvantageFlow (Kveton et al., 2026) regularizes
advantage-weighted regression through the rollout policy. We build on
the direct-regression approach, introducing within-utterance credit and
explicit positive/negative stopping thresholds instead of changing the
velocity target. GROW’s existing reference anchor is retained.

## 3 LoCA: Local Credit with Two-Sided Control

### 3.1 From sentence feedback to signed regression

For each text and reference-voice condition, generate $`G`$ candidates,
score their audio, and retain their latents for replay. Let
$`r_{i}^{c}=\exp(-2.5e_{i})`$ be the content reward and $`r_{i}^{s}`$
speaker similarity. Using group means and sample standard deviations,
define

```math
A_{i}=\frac{r_{i}^{c}-\mu_{c}}{\sigma_{c}+10^{-8}}+0.5\frac{r_{i}^{s}-\mu_{s}}{\sigma_{s}+10^{-8}}. \tag{1}
```

Separate normalization controls the reward scales; constant components
contribute zero.

For generated latents $`z`$ and Gaussian noise $`\epsilon`$, the
data-at-zero flow path (Lipman et al., 2023) is

```math
x_{\tau}=(1-\tau)z+\tau\epsilon,\qquad y=\epsilon-z. \tag{2}
```

The policy velocity $`v_{it}`$ and frozen pretrained reference
$`v^{\rm ref}_{it}`$ receive the same generated history, noisy input and
flow time. For channel dimension $`d`$, let

```math
\displaystyle E_{it} \displaystyle=\|v_{it}-y_{it}\|^{2}/d, \tag{3}
```

```math
\displaystyle E^{\rm ref}_{it} \displaystyle=\|v^{\rm ref}_{it}-y_{it}\|^{2}/d,
```

```math
\displaystyle D_{it} \displaystyle=\|v_{it}-v^{\rm ref}_{it}\|^{2}/d.
```

Following GROW (Yang et al., 2026), the sentence-weighted starting point
is

```math
\mathcal{L}_{\rm GROW}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,t}(A_{i}E_{it}+\beta D_{it}), \tag{4}
```

where $`N`$ counts generated frames. Its reward term has derivative
$`A_{i}`$ with respect to $`E_{it}`$ before averaging: the sign selects
fitting or repulsion, but every frame of an utterance shares that sign
and coefficient.

### 3.2 Constructing and applying local credit

Figure 1: Local-credit construction in an illustrative 12-frame example.
Six unflagged and four error frames give mean label 0.6; two unknown

0.05, error frames receive final coefficient -0.07, unflagged frames

#### From text errors to frame labels.

We transcribe each generated utterance with Qwen3-ASR (Shi et al., 2026)
and align the recognized words to its waveform with the forced aligner.
Edit alignment against the target text identifies substitutions and
insertions; their timestamps locate error spans. Deletions use
neighboring-word boundaries because omitted words have no emitted span.
These intervals are projected onto the exact generated latent-frame
timeline, rather than treated as acoustic tokens.

#### Availability-aware feedback.

Each frame receives $`q_{it}\in\{-1,0,1\}`$: unknown, detected error, or
unflagged. Unknown includes unreliable timing and the unmeasured tail;
it is not a negative reward. Confirmed anomalous silence is labeled 0,
while eligible exact-phonetic substitutions become unknown. Define
$`M_{it}=\mathbf{1}[q_{it}\geq 0]`$. We center only measured labels
within each complete utterance:

```math
\displaystyle\bar{q}_{i} \displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{t}M_{it}q_{it}}{\max(1,\sum_{t}M_{it})}, \tag{5}
```

```math
\displaystyle\delta_{it} \displaystyle=\alpha M_{it}(q_{it}-\bar{q}_{i}),\qquad a_{it}=A_{i}+\delta_{it}.
```

Error and unflagged frames receive relative negative and positive
increments; unknown frames receive zero. The increments sum to zero,
preserving the sentence coefficient’s mean. Uniform or wholly unknown
labels yield zero increments. Centering is per utterance, not across
candidates or microbatches.

#### Integration with regression.

We broadcast $`A_{i}`$ to the generated frames and replace it by
$`a_{it}`$ in the regression objective below. Labels and coefficients
are detached; prefix, padding and stop positions are excluded. No
separate local reward loss is added. The final coefficient need not
change sign, but can do so when local evidence outweighs the sentence
advantage (Figure 1). Appendix B specifies timing, deletion and
label-priority rules.

### 3.3 Two-sided thresholds for controlled updates

We next control how long either sign of regression remains active. With
positive budget $`m_{+}`$, negative margin $`m_{-}`$ and relative
fraction $`0<\rho\leq 1`$, define

```math
\displaystyle b^{+}_{it} \displaystyle=\min(m_{+},\rho E^{\rm ref}_{it}), \tag{6}
```

```math
\displaystyle\ell_{it} \displaystyle=E^{\rm ref}_{it}-b^{+}_{it},\qquad u_{it}=E^{\rm ref}_{it}+m_{-}.
```

The relative budget keeps the positive floor nonnegative even when
reference error is small. Using $`[x]_{+}=\max(x,0)`$, the combined
objective is

```math
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,t}\big( \displaystyle[a_{it}]_{+}[E_{it}-\ell_{it}]_{+} \tag{7}
```

```math
\displaystyle+[-a_{it}]_{+}[u_{it}-E_{it}]_{+}+\beta D_{it}\big).
```

Feedback, targets and reference predictions are detached. Each active
branch retains the signed-regression derivative: positive fitting stops
at the improvement budget, and negative repulsion stops at the upper
threshold. The reference penalty continues to anchor both branches. This
removes the incentive for unbounded negative regression and limits
repeated positive fitting. Appendix B.1 derives the gradients and
fixed-replay bounds; these properties concern the regression objective
rather than a global bound on parameter drift.

### 4.1 Backbone, data and evaluation

All systems use the publicly released 2B-parameter VoxCPM2 checkpoint
(openbmb/VoxCPM2; OpenBMB, 2026). The base is evaluated without RL;
GROW, LoCA and the ablations start from this same checkpoint.

RL samples a 50,000-example Emilia (He et al., 2024) pool covering
Chinese, English, German, French, Japanese and Korean. Hyperparameters
are in Appendix A. Evaluation uses seven CV3 (QwenAudio, 2026) language
splits (Chinese, English, German, Spanish, French, Italian and Russian;
500 each), plus hard Chinese/English (60/64), totaling 3,624. This is a
fixed subset of CV3. Seed-TTS-Eval (ByteDance Speech, 2024) includes
Chinese hard (400) and standard Chinese/English (2,020/1,088).

We select representative models by jointly considering content accuracy,
severe failures and speaker similarity. Complete training trajectories
and supplementary results are provided in Appendix C.

### 4.2 Automatic severe failure rate

For item $`j`$, let $`e_{j}`$ be utterance CER/WER; $`S_{j}`$, $`C_{j}`$
and $`X_{j}`$ indicate all-silent output, a generation-cap hit and a
generation error. Let $`T_{j}`$ and $`I_{j}`$ be tail and longest
internal silence in seconds:

```math
\displaystyle F_{j} \displaystyle=\mathbf{1}[e_{j}>0.5\lor S_{j}\lor C_{j}\lor X_{j} \tag{8}
```

```math
\displaystyle\lor T_{j}\geq 1.2\lor I_{j}\geq 1.5],
```

```math
\displaystyle\mathrm{SFR} \displaystyle=\frac{100}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}F_{j}.
```

The denominator $`M`$ is the full evaluation set, including generation
errors. Each flagged item counts once; tables and plots report this
union count as severe proxy cases. Detection details are in Appendix D.

### 4.3 Main results and ablations

LoCA achieves CV3 error 4.758%, versus 5.986% for the base and 5.553%
for GROW (Table 1). Severe proxy cases decrease from 61/60 to 51, and
SIM rises from 67.168/67.562 to 69.137. On ordinary CV3, LoCA has error
4.673%, versus 5.887%/5.461%. It also lowers Seed-hard CER to 6.884%
with 5 severe cases. CV3-hard error is 7.163%, with 6 cases: lower error
than both baselines, with the base’s case count and one fewer case than
GROW.

Table 2 evaluates component removals. Without local credit, CV3 error is
5.508%, with 54 cases and SIM 67.525. Without both thresholds, it is
5.488%, with 44 cases and SIM 67.722. Full LoCA has better content
accuracy and speaker similarity than both variants; the no-threshold
variant has fewer severe cases. The trajectories retain these
metric-dependent trade-offs.

### 4.4 Russian generalization

Russian is excluded from the six-language RL pool. On its 500-item CV3
split, LoCA’s selected checkpoint reduces WER from the base’s 6.219% to
4.723% (24.1% relative) and raises SIM from 67.296 to 68.541; severe
proxy cases are 6 versus the base’s 5. WER remains below the base at
every evaluated LoCA checkpoint. This supports transfer beyond the RL
training languages; it does not imply Russian was absent from backbone
pretraining. Language profiles and similarity trajectories are in
Appendix E.

## 5 Conclusion

LoCA combines local credit with two-sided regression control for
continuous TTS. The selected model improves CV3 and Seed-hard content
accuracy over the baselines, with higher CV3 speaker similarity. Russian
results show content and similarity gains outside the RL training pool.
Full trajectories retain the cross-checkpoint and hard-set trade-offs.

## Limitations

Experiments use one training seed and one backbone. Results describe the
evaluated language and task subsets.

Local labels inherit recognition and timing errors. Phonetic matching is
a text-side proxy, while the severe-failure metric is an automatic
diagnostic. Fixed-replay threshold properties do not guarantee optimizer
convergence or absence of parameter drift.

## Ethical Considerations

Voice cloning can enable impersonation. Audio/model release requires
appropriate dataset permissions and speaker rights; this study does not
establish redistribution rights for every source recording. Automatic
error measures do not guarantee fairness across accents or dialects.

## Appendix A Training and Evaluation Protocol

#### Training.

All trained configurations start from the BF16 release of the
2B-parameter VoxCPM2 model (Zhou et al., 2026; OpenBMB, 2026); GROW
denotes its advantage-weighted objective applied to this backbone. The
50,000-example Emilia pool spans Chinese, English, German, French,
Japanese and Korean. Pool size is distinct from training duration, which
is specified by steps. We use group size 8, replay microbatches of two,
and two-condition gradient accumulation. AdamW (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019) uses learning rate $`2\times 10^{-6}`$, 50-step warmup, zero
weight decay, gradient clipping at 0.5 and seed 42. LoCA uses
$`\alpha=.2`$, $`m_{+}=m_{-}=.1`$, $`\rho=.5`$ and $`\beta=.025`$.

Training samples use ten flow steps, classifier-free guidance (Ho and
Salimans, 2022) scale 2 and a 20-second duration ceiling. Generated
latents are retained for replay. The audio autoencoder, patch encoder
and stop head are frozen; no supervised fine-tuning or stop loss is
added. Losses are averaged over generated frames. Content rewards use
Qwen3-ASR (Shi et al., 2026); speaker rewards use similarity to the
reference voice.

#### Evaluation.

All systems use reference-audio conditioning, ten flow steps, guidance
scale 2 and fixed per-example seeds, without retries or best-of-$`N`$
selection. CV3 includes only the seven ordinary splits and two hard
splits listed in the main text; it excludes Japanese/Korean and other
CV3 tasks. Russian, Spanish and Italian are outside the RL training
language pool.

Chinese uses a Paraformer-family recognizer (Gao et al., 2022); other
evaluated languages use Whisper large-v3 (Radford et al., 2023). Content
error is the untruncated mean of utterance CER/WER. CV3 aggregates these
rates by sample count. SIM is speaker-embedding cosine similarity times
100, using ERes2Net for CV3 (Chen et al., 2023) and the benchmark’s
fine-tuned WavLM verifier for Seed (Chen et al., 2022; ByteDance Speech,
2024). DNSMOS-P808 is an auxiliary automatic quality estimate (Reddy et
al., 2021), not human MOS.

## Appendix B Local Labels and Objective Properties

The alignment procedure compares normalized reference and ASR
transcripts, then uses timestamps aligned to the ASR hypothesis. For
waveform duration $`D`$ and $`L`$ generated latent frames, an error
interval from $`s`$ to $`e`$ maps to frame indices from
$`\lfloor sL/D\rfloor`$ to $`\lceil eL/D\rceil`$ (end exclusive),
clipped to the generated range. The mask contains exactly $`L`$
generated-frame entries.

Measured regions start at label 1; substitutions and insertions
overwrite their aligned spans with 0. Interior deletions mark the
boundary between adjacent recognized tokens, using an 80-ms window when
no gap exists. Beginning/end deletions use an 80-ms window clipped to
the neighboring word. These are localization heuristics, not timestamps
of an emitted missing word. Frames beyond the final reliable timestamp
are unknown ($`-1`$). Invalid alignment, near-silent audio or
generation-cap hits disable ASR labels; independently confirmed silence
can still supply local evidence.

Silence labels require an anomalous-silence detection supported by
waveform energy. A 0.3-second grace interval avoids penalizing uncertain
boundaries. Only frames fully inside the retained silence interval are
marked 0. These acoustic labels override ASR labels, including
phonetic-neutral regions. The recognition/timestamp helpers build on
public GLM-TTS code (Zai Org, 2025); the within-utterance centered
construction is the method studied here.

Phonetic neutralization applies only to equal-length substitution chunks
with nonempty, exactly matching pronunciation sequences and valid
timestamps. Eligible error labels become unavailable, preserving
overlapping insertion/deletion penalties and acoustic overlays. Uniform
all-error labels have no within-utterance contrast and hence zero local
increment; sentence feedback may still update those candidates.

### B.1 Fixed-replay derivations

#### Mean-preserving local credit.

For an utterance with $`n_{i}=\sum_{t}M_{it}>0`$, substituting Eq. 5
gives

```math
\sum_{t}\delta_{it}=\alpha\left(\sum_{t}M_{it}q_{it}-n_{i}\bar{q}_{i}\right)=0. \tag{9}
```

When $`n_{i}=0`$, every increment is zero by the mask. If the fraction
of unflagged measured frames is $`p`$, error and unflagged frames
receive increments $`-\alpha p`$ and $`\alpha(1-p)`$, respectively. This
preserves the mean coefficient before thresholding; different residuals
and network Jacobians prevent interpreting it as gradient conservation.

#### Why an anchor alone need not bound signed regression.

Fix one replay frame, its coefficient $`a`$, target $`y`$ and reference
$`v^{\rm ref}`$. Expanding the unaveraged signed loss yields

```math
\displaystyle L_{\rm signed}(v) \displaystyle=a\|v-y\|^{2}/d+\beta\|v-v^{\rm ref}\|^{2}/d \tag{10}
```

```math
\displaystyle=\frac{a+\beta}{d}\|v\|^{2}
```

```math
\displaystyle-\frac{2}{d}v^{\top}(ay+\beta v^{\rm ref})+C,
```

where $`C`$ is independent of $`v`$. If $`a+\beta<0`$, the quadratic
tends to negative infinity as the output norm grows. This is a
sufficient condition for a problematic frame objective, not a claim that
every GROW update diverges.

#### Thresholded derivatives.

Let $`h(E)=[a]_{+}[E-\ell]_{+}+[-a]_{+}[u-E]_{+}`$ be the unaveraged RL
term. Away from the hinge boundaries,

```math
\frac{\partial h}{\partial E}=\begin{cases}a\,\mathbf{1}[E>\ell],&a>0,\\
a\,\mathbf{1}[E<u],&a<0,\\
0,&a=0.\end{cases} \tag{11}
```

Hence each active branch matches signed regression, and its RL gradient
vanishes after saturation. Because $`\nabla_{v}E=2(v-y)/d`$, the active
negative branch satisfies

```math
\|\nabla_{v}h\|=2|a|\sqrt{E/d}\leq 2|a|\sqrt{(E^{\rm ref}+m_{-})/d}. \tag{12}
```

All terms of the thresholded loss are nonnegative. For $`\beta>0`$, the
per-frame total also satisfies $`L(v)\geq\beta\|v-v^{\rm ref}\|^{2}/d`$,
so it grows as the output moves arbitrarily far from the fixed
reference. The positive branch is not upper-bounded in error. These
statements concern a fixed replay output; the hinge is nonsmooth at its
boundary, and shared parameters can move a saturated frame again. They
establish control of the regression terms, not optimizer convergence or
globally drift-free training.
