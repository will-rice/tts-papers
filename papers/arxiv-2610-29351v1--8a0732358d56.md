# Closed-Loop Dual-Reference Guidance for Accent Control in Cross-Lingual Voice Cloning

Yuanzhong Zheng    Wei Huang    Ming Ma    Kailun Jin    Youhai
Jiang\sthanksCorresponding author

###### Abstract

Zero-shot cross-lingual voice cloning often carries the reference
speaker’s accent into the target language. We propose closed-loop
dual-reference guidance, which pairs the original reference with a
reusable native anchor constructed by voice conversion (VC). The
references initialize separate language-model (LM) states that guide a
shared acoustic trajectory; each completed patch then updates both
states, keeping their generated histories aligned with the emitted
speech. Anchor construction runs once per speaker, and online synthesis
uses a frozen TTS model without per-utterance VC. On 20 held-out French
speakers, the fixed setting $`\lambda^{\!*}=0.33`$ reduces residual
French LID mass by 73% versus direct cloning, at an ECAPA similarity
cost of only $`0.042`$; relative to anchor-only cloning, it recovers
$`0.155`$. The method achieves the lowest mean WER ($`0.009`$) and
highest mean GOP ($`4.750`$) among the held-out test arms. In blinded
preference tests, native-English listeners favor it over direct cloning
on pronunciation naturalness, and over both anchor-only cloning and a
TTS$`\to`$VC cascade on speaker identity. Online RTF is approximately
26% lower than the cascade’s ($`0.59`$ vs. $`0.80`$), and the same
setting reduces Spanish LID mass by 56% without re-tuning.

## 1 Introduction

Cross-lingual zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) synthesizes a target
language from a short reference in another language. The reference
carries both vocal identity and pronunciation patterns. A French
reference, for example, can yield intelligible English with a French
accent. Accent control must therefore regulate how reference
pronunciation influences synthesis while retaining speaker identity.

Accent control can be learned during training or applied during
synthesis. Disentanglement methods use multilingual or multi-accented
data and task-specific optimization \[1, 2, 3, 4\]; a
TTS$`\to`$voice-conversion (VC) cascade instead converts every output
utterance. For frozen models, Activation Steering \[5\] uses
offline-extracted accent directions, while standard classifier-free
guidance (CFG) and its flow-matching adaptations \[6, 7, 8\] mix
conditional and unconditional velocity fields. We use a native speech
reference to supply the pronunciation condition and reuse it across
utterances, placing accent control inside autoregressive generation.

Our method uses two references with distinct, complementary roles: the
original speech anchors speaker identity, while a converted native
anchor supplies target-language pronunciation. Both references condition
the same generated patch, but mixing their decoder outputs alone leaves
open how the two autoregressive states should advance. We feed the
emitted patch back to both states, so each branch follows the speech
actually produced while retaining its own reference context. The weight
$`\lambda`$ controls the anchor’s contribution, and the same anchor is
reused for subsequent utterances.

Contributions:

1.  1.  Closed-loop autoregressive composition. We address context
        desynchronization: independently advanced reference branches
        condition guidance on different generated histories. Feeding each
        emitted patch back to both LM states preserves a common history
        while retaining distinct reference contexts. On development, this
        update reduces WER from $`0.139`$ with independent branch rollouts
        to $`0.004`$ and raises GOP from $`3.524`$ to $`4.791`$.
2.  2.  Reusable native anchors. Zero-shot VC constructs a speaker-specific
        native reference at enrollment, without parallel bilingual
        recordings or task-specific training. Subsequent utterances reuse
        this reference, moving conversion out of online synthesis.
3.  3.  Composition in velocity and conditioning space. The shared state
        update supports both CFG-Zero\* \[9\] velocity mixing and fusion of
        decoder conditions. Conditioning fusion uses one conditional branch
        instead of two, reducing decoder branch evaluations by one third
        ($`27`$ to $`18`$ per patch) while retaining both reference contexts
        and their feedback.

## 2 Related Work

Accent conversion and post-hoc steering. Earlier work maps non-native
speech toward a native target \[10, 11\], uses phonetic
posteriorgrams \[12, 13\], or factorizes accent-related attributes in a
neural codec \[14\]. Activation Steering \[5\] extracts layer-wise
accent directions without further training. Our guidance comes from a
speaker-specific native anchor and two reference contexts updated with
the generated speech. Vevo \[15\] separates style and timbre through
trained modules. We use Seed-VC \[16\] for both anchor construction and
the TTS$`\to`$VC baseline, comparing a reusable enrollment-time
reference with per-utterance conversion.

Cross-lingual TTS and guidance formulations. Zhang et al. \[17\] studied
accent leakage in cross-lingual TTS, followed by training-time
disentanglement methods \[2, 3, 4, 18\]. X-Voice \[1\] combines
pseudo-parallel prompts for training-time adaptation with
dropped-condition CFG, $`v(\mathrm{ref})-v(\emptyset)`$.
CrossAccent-TTS \[4\] and Accent Vector \[18\] learn representations or
weights for accent control. Our method composes two
reference-conditioned states during inference in a frozen TTS model.
Dual-scale CFG methods vary guidance between text and speaker
conditions \[19, 20, 21\]; our two contexts each contain a speech
reference and follow a common generated history. Section 6.1 compares
native-anchor guidance with a reference-free secondary context.

## 3 Method

### 3.1 Synthetic native anchor

Let $`x`$ denote target-language text, and let a frozen zero-shot TTS
condition its flow-matching decoder on reference speech $`r`$, yielding
$`v(z,t;x,r)`$. For each speaker we synthesize a native-English anchor
$`r_{\mathrm{EN}}`$ with a zero-shot voice converter (Seed-VC) \[16\],
converting a native English donor toward the timbre of the source
reference $`r_{\mathrm{FR}}`$.

Donors are a curated LibriTTS-R \[27\] subset (highest-DNSMOS utterance
from each of the top 20 native English speakers). With a fixed seed,
four distinct donors are cyclically assigned per speaker. Seed-VC with
length adjustment $`1.0`$ preserves donor duration
($`3.04`$–$`9.92`$ s). We keep the candidate maximizing
$`\mathrm{SIM}_{\mathrm{avg}}=(\mathrm{SIM}_{\mathrm{ECAPA}}+\mathrm{SIM}_{\mathrm{CAM++\text{-}CN}})/2`$
against $`r_{\mathrm{FR}}`$. The four conversions are performed at
enrollment; the selected anchor is reused for subsequent utterances
(Fig. 1).

### 3.2 Closed-loop dual-reference guidance

Generation has an outer autoregressive patch index $`k`$ and an inner
flow time $`t`$. Let $`H_{X,k}`$ denote the base- and residual-LM state,
including their KV caches, for reference context
$`X\in\{\mathrm{FR},\mathrm{EN}\}`$. Each context is initialized by
jointly prefilling the target text and local-encoder features of
$`r_{X}`$. At step $`k`$, the LMs provide diffusion-transformer (DiT)
conditioning $`\mu_{X,k}`$, which remains fixed during that patch’s ODE
solve. Both fields are evaluated on the same evolving latent $`z_{k,t}`$
and combined with CFG-Zero\* \[9\]. Suppressing $`k`$ for clarity,
define $`u_{X}=v(z,t;x,\mu_{X})`$ and the shared unconditional velocity
$`u_{0}=v(z,t;x,\emptyset)`$. With stabilizer $`\epsilon=10^{-8}`$ and
guidance scale $`s=2.0`$,

```math
\left\{\begin{aligned} \alpha_{X}&=\frac{\langle u_{X},u_{0}\rangle}{\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\epsilon},\\
\widehat{v}_{X}&=\alpha_{X}u_{0}+s\bigl(u_{X}-\alpha_{X}u_{0}\bigr),\\
v_{\lambda}&=(1-\lambda)\,\widehat{v}_{\mathrm{FR}}+\lambda\,\widehat{v}_{\mathrm{EN}}.\end{aligned}\right. \tag{1}
```

CFG-Zero\* splits the conditional field into components parallel and
orthogonal to $`u_{0}`$ via $`\alpha_{X}`$. The first Euler update is
zeroed (fraction $`0.04`$); the remaining nine follow Eq. (1). Sampling
is fixed (sway $`1.0`$, temperature $`1.0`$, 10 Euler steps).

After the ODE produces acoustic patch $`Z_{k}`$, its local encoding and
projection $`E(Z_{k})`$ advance both LM contexts:

```math
H_{X,k+1}=F\bigl(H_{X,k},E(Z_{k})\bigr),\qquad X\in\{\mathrm{FR},\mathrm{EN}\}. \tag{2}
```

Here $`F`$ is the frozen base- and residual-LM update. Both contexts
follow the emitted history while retaining distinct reference prefills.
The open-KV ablation uses independently sampled histories (Sec. 6.1).

The same update supports a conditioning-space implementation:
$`\mu\leftarrow(1-\lambda)\mu_{\mathrm{FR}}+\lambda\mu_{\mathrm{EN}}`$.
This variant retains both contexts and Eq. (2), then uses a single
conditional DiT branch plus the unconditional branch (Secs. 5.3, 6.1).
Velocity mixing is used for all held-out test and transfer runs.
$`\lambda=0`$ gives A, $`\lambda=1`$ gives B, and intermediate values
give C. We swept $`\lambda\in\{0.1,0.2,0.25,0.33,0.5,0.75\}`$ on
development data and fixed $`\lambda^{\!*}=0.33`$ before evaluating the
test set; throughout, $`\lambda^{\!*}`$ denotes this selected operating
point rather than an optimized value.

## 5 Results

### 5.1 Development trade-off and $`\lambda^{*}`$ selection

At $`\lambda=0.33`$, the GOP gain over A is $`+0.189`$, nearly matching
the $`+0.193`$ attained at $`\lambda=0.5`$, while the ECAPA similarity
cost is roughly halved ($`\Delta\mathrm{ECAPA}=-0.049`$
\[$`-0.063,-0.036`$\]; Fig. 2). CAM++-EN follows the same trend. At this
setting, paired differences are $`+0.021`$ \[$`-0.062,0.098`$\] in GOP
relative to B and $`-0.018`$ \[$`-0.068,0.032`$\] in DNSMOS relative to
the cascade. We selected $`\lambda^{\!*}=0.33`$ on development and fixed
it for test and Spanish transfer.

### 5.2 Held-out test evaluation ($`\lambda^{*}`$ frozen)

C vs. A. At the fixed operating point, C achieves mean WER $`0.009`$ and
GOP $`4.750`$, the lowest and highest values, respectively, among the
held-out test arms (Table 1). Relative to direct cloning, it reduces
residual French LID mass by 73% ($`0.093\to 0.025`$; $`\Delta=-0.068`$
\[$`-0.119,-0.027`$\]) and increases GOP by $`0.087`$
\[$`0.048,0.128`$\] (Fig. 3). The paired WER difference is $`-0.0072`$
\[$`-0.0147,0.0003`$\], with an ECAPA change of $`-0.042`$
\[$`-0.048,-0.036`$\].

C vs. B. Adding the original-reference branch recovers $`0.155`$ ECAPA
similarity (\[$`0.137,0.173`$\]) and $`0.142`$ CAM++-EN similarity
(\[$`0.122,0.162`$\]) over anchor-only cloning. This speaker-similarity
recovery comes with higher mean GOP ($`4.750`$ vs. $`4.733`$) and lower
mean WER ($`0.009`$ vs. $`0.013`$); paired differences are $`+0.017`$
\[$`-0.043,0.072`$\] and $`-0.004`$ \[$`-0.011,0.002`$\].

### 5.3 Comparison with the TTS$`\to`$VC cascade

On the item-matched three-seed test set ($`n=1200`$/arm), C reduces mean
WER from the cascade’s $`0.047`$ to $`0.009`$, an approximately 81%
reduction ($`\Delta=-0.038`$ \[$`-0.046,-0.031`$\]), and raises GOP from
$`4.378`$ to $`4.750`$ ($`\Delta=+0.372`$ \[$`0.330,0.412`$\]). Word
repetition or insertion involving at least two words occurs in $`3.2\%`$
($`38/1200`$) of cascade outputs, versus less than $`0.5\%`$ for A, B,
and C.

Speaker similarity also improves over the cascade: ECAPA increases by
$`0.091`$ (95% CI \[$`0.073,0.109`$\]) and CAM++-EN by $`0.094`$
(\[$`0.073,0.114`$\]). In the blinded development study, listeners
likewise prefer C on speaker identity ($`38`$ vs. $`5`$ sessions,
$`p<0.0001`$; Sec. 5.5). On the remaining metrics, C trails the cascade:
C$`-`$cascade is $`+0.024`$ on residual $`P(\mathrm{fr})`$
(\[$`0.005,0.052`$\]; cascade mean $`0.001`$), $`-0.139`$ on DNSMOS
(\[$`-0.197,-0.089`$\]), and $`-0.022`$ on CAM++-CN (CI
\[$`-0.039,-0.006`$\]; Sec. 6.2). Thus, the cascade’s higher DNSMOS
accompanies more word errors, while C improves both transcription
accuracy and listener-rated speaker identity.

### 5.4 Cross-language generalization (Spanish$`\to`$English)

The French-selected $`\lambda^{\!*}=0.33`$ transfers directly to 15
Spanish speakers without language-specific tuning. Residual Spanish LID
mass drops from $`0.032`$ to $`0.014`$ ($`\Delta=-0.018`$
\[$`-0.026,-0.012`$\]; Fig. 3), and ECAPA similarity recovers from
$`0.323`$ to $`0.474`$ relative to B. C achieves WER $`0.0055`$,
compared with $`0.0054`$ for A ($`\Delta=+0.0001`$
\[$`-0.0070,0.0065`$\]). The same operating point therefore combines
source-LID reduction with speaker-similarity recovery in both
French-to-English and Spanish-to-English cloning.

## 6 Analysis

### 6.1 Guidance mechanisms and architectural ablations

Shared autoregressive feedback. The open-KV ablation tests the effect of
independently advancing the reference branches. Both configurations mix
velocities for the emitted patch, but open-KV feeds each LM its own
separately sampled, unmixed prediction. This permits context
desynchronization: each branch follows its own generated history rather
than the emitted sequence. Replacing these independent rollouts with
shared patch feedback reduces WER from $`0.139`$ to $`0.004`$ and raises
GOP from $`3.524`$ to $`4.791`$. Equation (2) enforces the
common-history constraint at every patch while preserving each branch’s
reference context.

Reference contexts. C (velocity) gives ECAPA $`0.467`$ versus $`0.372`$
for concat; concat reaches $`P(\mathrm{fr})=0.003`$. Replacing the
native anchor with $`\emptyset`$ lowers similarity and GOP. The
waveforms used by wavavg differ in length and phase; their average gives
GOP $`3.131`$.

Composition across mixing sites. C (conditioning) achieves ECAPA
$`0.465`$ and GOP $`4.828`$, versus $`0.467`$ and $`4.791`$ for C
(velocity). The paired differences are not significant (velocity minus
conditioning: ECAPA $`+0.003`$ \[$`-0.004,0.009`$\]; GOP $`-0.038`$
\[$`-0.089,0.014`$\]). Both implementations retain separate reference
states and common patch feedback; conditioning mixing achieves RTF
$`0.572`$ (Table 2).

### 6.2 Encoder-domain discrepancy in speaker verification

The three encoders assign substantially different similarity changes to
the same accent-guided speech. On French development, C vs. A at
$`\lambda=0.5`$ is $`-0.111`$ on ECAPA and $`-0.104`$ on CAM++-EN, but
$`-0.020`$ on CAM++-CN. The CAM++ checkpoints share an architecture but
were trained on VoxCeleb2 and a 200k-speaker Chinese dataset,
respectively; their responses show that the estimated identity cost of
accent control depends on the verification model. At
$`\lambda^{\!*}=0.33`$, the CAM++-CN difference is $`+0.003`$
\[$`-0.003,0.008`$\]. CAM++-CN also serves as Seed-VC’s internal speaker
encoder and contributes to our anchor-selection score (Sec. 3.1). On the
held-out test, ECAPA and CAM++-EN rank C above the cascade, whereas
CAM++-CN reverses that ordering. The blinded listening study
independently favors C on speaker identity ($`38`$ vs. $`5`$ sessions;
Sec. 5.5), aligning with the ECAPA and CAM++-EN ranking.

## 7 Conclusion and Limitations

We presented closed-loop dual-reference guidance for accent control in
frozen TTS. Separate reference states combine original-speaker
information with a reusable native anchor, and feedback from the emitted
patches keeps both branches on the same generated history; this
common-history constraint, rather than the choice of mixing site,
accounts for the largest quality gain (Sec. 6.1). On held-out French
speakers, the method cuts residual French LID mass by 73% versus direct
cloning while recovering $`0.155`$ ECAPA similarity over anchor-only
cloning, and the French-selected operating point transfers to Spanish
without re-tuning. Native-English listeners prefer C on pronunciation
naturalness over A and on speaker identity over B and the cascade. The
current evaluation targets English only, anchor quality depends on the
zero-shot converter, and the guidance weight is fixed across patches;
extending the evaluation to additional target languages, adapting
guidance per patch, and improving converted-anchor quality are the next
steps.
