# Joint Residual Reweighting for Classifier-Free Guidance in Flow-Matching Zero-Shot TTS

###### Abstract

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is widely used in flow-matching-based
zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS), where generation is conditioned on text
content and a speech prompt. Standard CFG uses a single guidance weight
for their joint conditional effect, while branch-selective guidance
emphasizes text or speaker conditioning and can introduce a trade-off
between text accuracy and speaker similarity. In this paper, we revisit
CFG under independently masked conditions and decompose the guidance
field into text, speaker, and joint residuals. We show that
condition-specific branch differences couple the joint residual with the
corresponding text or speaker residual under a shared weight. Trajectory
analysis further shows that the joint residual varies over flow time and
contains information that cannot be represented by reweighting the text
and speaker residuals alone. Based on these observations, we propose
joint residual reweighting, which assigns independent weights to the
three residuals. Experiments on F5-TTS, CosyVoice2, and GLM-TTS across
three evaluation sets show overall improvements in speaker similarity
and text accuracy over the default CFG settings without retraining.

zero-shot text-to-speech, flow matching, classifier-free guidance,
multi-condition generation

## 1 Introduction

Zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) aims to synthesize speech for an unseen
speaker from a short reference utterance. The generated speech should
follow the target text while preserving the reference speaker’s voice.
Recent flow-matching-based TTS systems model speech generation through a
conditional flow-matching process with text and reference speech, and
have shown strong zero-shot performance \[2, 6, 9, 11\].

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is widely used in these models to control
the strength of conditional information during inference. Standard CFG
strengthens the joint effect of text and speaker conditioning, but does
not provide separate control over the two. Recent studies have therefore
explored condition-specific guidance by constructing additional branches
with one condition masked and using branch differences as guidance
directions \[8, 12, 13\]. These approaches provide finer control, but
also expose a trade-off between text accuracy and speaker similarity:
stronger speaker-oriented guidance can improve speaker similarity while
degrading text accuracy \[13\].

A key limitation of this branch-difference view is that each difference
is treated as a single direction. Our analysis suggests that this
trade-off is related to the fact that the effects of the two conditions
are not fully independent: a speaker-oriented branch can also affect
text-related information, and vice versa. We hypothesize that this
cross-condition interference is caused by a component that emerges only
when text and speaker conditions are jointly present. This interaction
can be viewed as a _joint residual_ component, which is not explicitly
modeled by existing condition-specific guidance. By considering the four
branches obtained by independently masking the two conditions, we
isolate this joint residual and find that it is non-negligible, varies
over flow time, and cannot in general be represented by rescaling the
text and speaker residuals.

Based on this observation, we introduce joint residual reweighting,
which explicitly decomposes the guidance field into text, speaker, and
joint residuals and reweights them independently. This provides direct
control over the joint component without retraining or additional solver
steps. Experiments on F5-TTS \[2\], CosyVoice2 \[5\], and GLM-TTS \[4\]
with LibriSpeech-PC, SEED-EN, and SEED-ZH show improvements in both
content accuracy and speaker similarity over default CFG settings.
Applying the method only at selected flow steps further retains these
gains with fewer additional branch evaluations.

## 2 Method

### 2.1 Text–speaker residual factorization

We consider a flow-matching TTS model with velocity predictor $`f`$. At
a given sampling state $`x_{t}`$ and flow time $`t`$, the generator is
conditioned on content information $`c_{\mathrm{text}}`$ and speaker
information $`c_{\mathrm{spk}}`$. Keeping $`(x_{t},t)`$ fixed and
independently masking the two conditions gives four velocity
predictions:

```math
\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{null}} \displaystyle=f(x_{t},t,\varnothing_{\mathrm{spk}},\varnothing_{\mathrm{text}}),
```

```math
\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{text}} \displaystyle=f(x_{t},t,\varnothing_{\mathrm{spk}},c_{\mathrm{text}}),
```

```math
\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{spk}} \displaystyle=f(x_{t},t,c_{\mathrm{spk}},\varnothing_{\mathrm{text}}),
```

```math
\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{full}} \displaystyle=f(x_{t},t,c_{\mathrm{spk}},c_{\mathrm{text}}). \tag{1}
```

They denote the unconditional, text-only, speaker-only, and fully
conditioned predictions, respectively.

Taking $`v_{\mathrm{null}}`$ as a common reference, the isolated text
and speaker effects are

```math
T=v_{\mathrm{text}}-v_{\mathrm{null}},\qquad S=v_{\mathrm{spk}}-v_{\mathrm{null}}. \tag{2}
```

Here, $`T`$ measures the response to adding the text condition without
speaker conditioning, while $`S`$ measures the response to adding the
speaker condition without text conditioning. These differences
correspond to the null-based condition directions used in separated
multi-condition CFG.

If text and speaker conditioning were purely additive, the fully
conditioned prediction would satisfy

```math
v_{\mathrm{full}}=v_{\mathrm{null}}+T+S. \tag{3}
```

Joint conditioning may, however, produce a response beyond this additive
combination. We define the deviation as

```math
\displaystyle I \displaystyle=(v_{\mathrm{full}}-v_{\mathrm{null}})-T-S
```

```math
\displaystyle=v_{\mathrm{full}}-v_{\mathrm{text}}-v_{\mathrm{spk}}+v_{\mathrm{null}}. \tag{4}
```

We refer to $`I`$ as the joint residual, which captures the deviation of
joint text–speaker conditioning from the additive combination of the two
isolated effects. The fully conditioned prediction can therefore be
written as

```math
v_{\mathrm{full}}=v_{\mathrm{null}}+T+S+I. \tag{5}
```

This factorization also reveals how the joint residual enters
condition-specific guidance:

```math
v_{\mathrm{full}}-v_{\mathrm{text}}=S+I,\qquad v_{\mathrm{full}}-v_{\mathrm{spk}}=T+I. \tag{6}
```

Thus, a speaker-oriented branch difference couples $`S`$ with $`I`$,
while a text-oriented difference couples $`T`$ with $`I`$. A shared
guidance weight adjusts the two components together. This motivates us
to examine whether $`I`$ is substantial and whether it can be
represented by reweighting $`T`$ and $`S`$ alone.

### 2.2 Properties of the text–speaker interaction

We examine the joint residual $`I`$ along the default CFG trajectories,
without applying our reweighting. We focus on three questions: whether
$`I`$ is substantial, whether it can be represented by reweighting $`T`$
and $`S`$, and how it modifies the two condition effects.

Interaction magnitude. We first measure the magnitude of $`I`$ relative
to the full–null conditional direction:

```math
r_{I}=\frac{\lVert I\rVert_{2}}{\lVert v_{\mathrm{full}}-v_{\mathrm{null}}\rVert_{2}}. \tag{7}
```

A larger $`r_{I}`$ indicates a stronger deviation from additive
conditioning. Figure 1(a) shows a substantial joint residual in all
three systems, with distinct dynamics over flow time. F5-TTS approaches
one around the middle of sampling and remains high, whereas CosyVoice2
and GLM-TTS gradually decrease in later stages.

Non-redundancy. A substantial $`I`$ does not necessarily require an
independent guidance component, since it may still be represented by
reweighting $`T`$ and $`S`$. We therefore measure its energy outside
$`\mathrm{span}(T,S)`$. After flattening the velocity tensors over
frames, we obtain the least-squares projection
$`I_{\parallel}=a^{\star}T+b^{\star}S`$ and define

```math
r_{\perp}=\frac{\lVert I-I_{\parallel}\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\lVert I\rVert_{2}^{2}}. \tag{8}
```

Figure 1(b) shows substantial energy outside this span for all three
systems. Notably, CosyVoice2 and GLM-TTS exhibit higher $`r_{\perp}`$
than F5-TTS in the middle and later stages despite their smaller
$`r_{I}`$. Thus, the magnitude and non-redundancy of the joint residual
capture different properties.

Effect modification. Cosine similarity has been used to examine
directional alignment along CFG trajectories \[1\]. We examine how $`I`$
changes the text and speaker directions:

```math
a_{T}=\cos(T,T+I),\qquad a_{S}=\cos(S,S+I). \tag{9}
```

Values close to one indicate little directional change. Figure 1(c)
shows distinct effects across models and flow time. For F5-TTS, $`I`$
strongly alters the text direction in later stages while its effect on
the speaker direction is smaller. CosyVoice2 and GLM-TTS show different
profiles, with $`a_{S}`$ increasing over time and eventually exceeding
$`a_{T}`$.

Together, these results show that the joint residual is substantial, not
fully represented by $`T`$ and $`S`$, and affects the two condition
directions differently across models and flow time. This motivates
treating $`I`$ as an independent guidance component. These observations
also guide the model-specific coefficient selection in Sec. 3.1.

### 2.3 Joint residual reweighting for CFG

The preceding analysis motivates treating $`I`$ as an independent
guidance component. Under the factorization in Eq. (5), standard
full–null CFG can be written as

```math
\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{CFG}} \displaystyle=v_{\mathrm{full}}+r(v_{\mathrm{full}}-v_{\mathrm{null}})
```

```math
\displaystyle=v_{\mathrm{full}}+r(T+S+I), \tag{10}
```

where a single guidance weight jointly scales all three residuals.

We propose joint residual reweighting, which introduces an independent
guidance weight for each residual:

```math
\displaystyle v_{\mathrm{guided}}={} \displaystyle v_{\mathrm{full}}+g_{T}T+g_{S}S+g_{I}I. \tag{11}
```

Here, $`g_{T}`$, $`g_{S}`$, and $`g_{I}`$ control the additional text,
speaker, and joint guidance, respectively. Setting
$`g_{T}=g_{S}=g_{I}=r`$ recovers standard full–null CFG.

Standard CFG uses one guidance degree of freedom by jointly scaling
$`T`$, $`S`$, and $`I`$. Condition-specific guidance introduces two
degrees of freedom, but still couples $`I`$ with either $`T`$ or $`S`$.
In contrast, our formulation independently controls all three residuals
through $`g_{T}`$, $`g_{S}`$, and $`g_{I}`$.

The four prediction branches share the same $`(x_{t},t)`$ and can be
evaluated in one batch. Our method requires no retraining or additional
ODE solver steps; the additional computation comes from evaluating the
extra condition branches at guided steps. We discuss reducing this
additional computation in Sec. 3.3.

## 3 Experiments

### 3.1 Experimental setup and guidance configuration

Backbones. We evaluate three open-source flow-matching zero-shot TTS
systems with different conditioning structures. F5-TTS \[2\] directly
generates speech with a non-autoregressive flow matcher conditioned on
text and reference speech. CosyVoice2 \[5\] first predicts semantic
tokens with a language model, which are then passed to a flow-based
acoustic decoder together with speaker conditioning. GLM-TTS \[4\]
similarly uses an autoregressive speech-token model followed by a
flow-based decoder, with speaker information provided through its
conditioning pathway. These systems therefore cover both direct and
cascaded flow-matching TTS architectures with different forms of content
and speaker conditioning.

Evaluation. We evaluate on the complete LibriSpeech-PC (1,127
utterances), SEED-EN (1,088), and SEED-ZH (2,020) sets. Content accuracy
is measured by WER for English and CER for Chinese, using Whisper
large-v3  \[10\] and Paraformer \[7\], respectively. Speaker similarity
(SIM) is computed using WavLM speaker embeddings \[3\]. F5-TTS uses 32
flow steps, while CosyVoice2 and GLM-TTS use 10. Within each backbone,
we keep the checkpoint, prompts, random seeds, sampling schedule, and
evaluation setup fixed across guidance settings.

Guidance configuration. We use each backbone’s default CFG sampler as
the baseline and compute the trajectory statistics in Sec. 2.2 on its
unmodified trajectories. Each statistic is averaged over flow time,
weighted by solver intervals, and then across utterances. We define
$`\Delta_{\mathrm{align}}=\bar{a}_{T}-\bar{a}_{S}`$ to characterize the
relative effect of the joint residual on the two condition directions,
with negative values indicating stronger modification of the text
direction and positive values stronger modification of the speaker
direction.

Table 1 reports the trajectory statistics and final guidance weights
$`g_{T}`$, $`g_{S}`$, and $`g_{I}`$. We use $`\Delta_{\mathrm{align}}`$
as an empirical heuristic for configuring the residual weights. Since
$`I`$ already contributes to the more strongly modified direction,
further increasing the corresponding main effect may overemphasize it.
We therefore retain its default CFG weight and adjust the other
condition weight together with $`g_{I}`$. Accordingly, F5-TTS retains
the default text weight, while GLM-TTS retains the default speaker
weight. For CosyVoice2, the alignment difference is near zero, showing
no clear preference; we retain the default text weight. The remaining
coefficients are selected through a small grid search for each backbone.

### 3.2 Main results

Table 2 reports results on the three complete evaluation sets. Relative
to each backbone’s released default CFG, the proposed reweighting
increases SIM and reduces WER/CER in all backbone–dataset combinations.
The relative benefits for speaker similarity and text accuracy vary
across models and evaluation sets, while the direction of improvement
over the default configurations is consistent.

For F5-TTS and GLM-TTS, the proposed method improves both metrics over
both evaluated CFG settings on all three sets. CosyVoice2 follows the
same pattern on the two English sets. On SEED-ZH, it achieves higher SIM
but slightly higher CER than the higher-scale CFG setting, while still
improving both metrics over the released default. This is the only case
where content accuracy does not improve over the best tested standard
CFG result. Overall, these comparisons suggest that residual-specific
reweighting offers a useful alternative to adjusting a single global
guidance scale.

Selective CFG \[13\] provides the closest comparison. For GLM-TTS, the
proposed method improves both metrics over our reproduced selective
setting on all three sets. The quoted F5-TTS and CosyVoice2 results
generally combine higher SIM with higher WER/CER than the proposed
method, illustrating a different balance between speaker preservation
and text accuracy.

### 3.3 Ablation study and inference efficiency

The proposed residual factorization suggests that the text, speaker, and
joint components play different roles in the speaker–text trade-off.
Table 3 reports an ablation on F5-TTS/LibriSpeech-PC, with all settings
expressed in the residual coordinates of Eq. (11). The standard CFG
sampler uses scale $`r=2.0`$, corresponding to
$`(g_{T},g_{S},g_{I})=(2.0,2.0,2.0)`$. Increasing $`g_{S}`$ and
$`g_{I}`$ together improves speaker similarity while keeping WER nearly
unchanged, and further increasing $`g_{I}`$ yields an additional SIM
gain. In contrast, increasing the two isolated effects while leaving
$`g_{I}`$ unchanged substantially reduces SIM despite a lower WER. These
results suggest that the joint residual has a distinct role in the
guidance trade-off and benefits from separate control.

Full-step guidance uses four branches rather than two in standard CFG,
increasing computation even when batched. We therefore apply the
correction only at selected steps and retain the released CFG elsewhere.
Sparse schedules are evaluated on the complete F5-TTS/LibriSpeech-PC
set, with all settings retaining 32 solver steps. In Table 4, _Loc._
denotes the corrected flow-step interval in start:end notation, with the
end index excluded, and _Branch Evals._ counts the total
condition-branch predictions across all solver steps. Concentrating the
correction in earlier steps is already effective: six corrected steps
improve both metrics with 76 branch evaluations, reducing the additional
evaluations over standard CFG from 64 to 12 (81.25%). Among the tested
schedules, eight corrected steps yield the lowest WER, while 16 yield
the highest SIM and match the baseline WER. Branch counts do not
directly measure runtime savings, and since the intervals are selected
on the same evaluation set, the results describe the observed trade-off
rather than held-out schedule generalization.

## 4 Conclusion

Classifier-free guidance is widely used in flow-matching zero-shot TTS,
but condition-specific guidance can couple individual and joint effects.
We decompose the guidance field into text, speaker, and joint residuals,
and introduce joint residual reweighting for independent control.
Experiments across three TTS systems and three evaluation sets show
overall gains in both content accuracy and speaker similarity without
retraining. Sparse-step guidance retains these gains with fewer parallel
branch evaluations.
